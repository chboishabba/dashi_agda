# Save Woogaroo Forest — carbon replacement and forest-maturity note

## Why this matters

A planted or regenerating offset can grow quickly without replacing what is lost at the impact site immediately.

There are several different questions that must not be collapsed into one:

1. How much carbon is stored in the vegetation and soils now?
2. How quickly are the existing trees and forest still accumulating carbon?
3. How quickly could an offset accumulate carbon?
4. When would the offset recover the carbon stock lost at the impact site?
5. When would it recover mature habitat structure and ecological function?
6. When would it support the same wildlife population and movement functions?

There may be a different answer — or no demonstrated answer — for each question.

## What the science says

Large old trees are not simply inert carbon stores. A global analysis found that absolute mass and carbon accumulation commonly increases with tree size. That does not mean every old forest has a higher annual stand-level carbon sink than every young forest.

Old forests can hold very large carbon stocks and can continue accumulating carbon, although the magnitude of old-growth net carbon sinks is scientifically debated. In an Australian montane-ash case study, old-growth forest held substantially more biomass carbon than logged forest; that ratio must not be transferred to Woogaroo without local measurement.

Young and restored forests can grow rapidly. Fast growth, however, does not mean the carbon stock that was removed has already been repaid. Restoration literature also shows that vegetation structure and biodiversity can take a long time to recover and do not automatically reach old-growth reference conditions.

For EPBC offsets, Commonwealth guidance already treats **time until ecological benefit**, future habitat quality, risk of loss and confidence as explicit inputs. The question is therefore not simply whether the offset contains more hectares or whether new trees can be planted.

## What counsel/ecologists should ask

For the impact site and each final offset parcel, obtain or estimate separately:

- current live and dead biomass/carbon stock;
- structural maturity: large trees, canopy structure, hollows, coarse woody debris and established food/resource trees;
- current habitat quality for each protected matter;
- realistic vegetation-growth and restoration trajectory;
- time until ecological benefit under the actual management plan;
- present and future ability to support Koala movement, feeding, refuge and breeding/dispersal;
- current and future connectivity to the relevant population;
- uncertainty and failure risk.

Then compare at least three separate replacement clocks:

- **carbon-stock parity** — when the offset stores as much carbon as was lost;
- **habitat-function parity** — when it provides comparable ecological structure/function;
- **population-support parity** — when it can support the same population/connectivity role.

Do not assume these occur at the same time.

## Relevance to the legal lanes

### EPBC offset adequacy

The strongest direct relevance is to offset maturity and restoration lag. Carbon is supporting evidence, but EPBC biodiversity-offset adequacy turns on the protected environmental attribute and conservation outcome, not carbon alone.

### Nature Conservation Act s 13

This helps test whether a proposed replacement habitat is genuinely substitutable for the existing habitat in the period that matters to the viable population. A future planted forest may not be a present substitute for mature connected habitat.

### Nature Conservation Act s 102

Long replacement times and loss of mature structure can inform the likely duration and reversibility of ecological effects. They do not themselves establish that the statutory s 102 test is met.

## Important cautions

- Large old tree ≠ every old stand has the highest annual sequestration rate.
- Fast young-forest growth ≠ lost carbon stock has been replaced.
- Equal carbon ≠ equal habitat.
- Equal hectares ≠ equal ecological function.
- Planted forest ≠ mature forest.
- Carbon parity ≠ restored carrying capacity or gene flow.
- General scientific literature ≠ Woogaroo-specific measurement.
