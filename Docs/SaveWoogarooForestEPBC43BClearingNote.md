# Save Woogaroo Forest — EPBC section 43B clearing note

## Why this matters

Section 43B of the Commonwealth *Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999* contains an exemption for some lawful continuations of land use that pre-date the EPBC Act.

That exemption was narrowed from **1 December 2025**.

For vegetation clearing, the continuation-of-use exemption cannot be relied on where the land has **not been cleared for at least 15 years**, unless the action is a forestry operation. There is also a separate restriction for clearing within 50 metres of certain waterways, wetlands or drainage lines in Great Barrier Reef catchments.

## What this does not prove

The 15-year rule does **not** automatically mean:

- clearing is prohibited;
- EPBC approval is automatically required;
- EPBC 2019/8575 must be refused; or
- a local planning approval is invalid.

It means that, if someone seeks to rely on the old section 43B continuation-of-use exemption for a particular clearing action, the recent clearing history of that land matters.

## Why it may be relevant at Woogaroo / Springview

The Springview / Woogaroo material includes mature and long-established vegetation, historical aerial material and a development history extending over many years. That makes the 15-year clearing-history question worth checking for any present or future clearing area where a continuation-of-use exemption might be asserted.

This is separate from the main EPBC 2019/8575 Part 9 merits decision. The project is already a referred controlled action, so section 43B should not be treated as a substitute for the live approval/refusal analysis.

## What counsel should check

For each clearing area where section 43B could conceivably be relied on:

1. What exact clearing action is proposed?
2. What land use is said to have continued from before the EPBC Act commenced?
3. Has that exact area been cleared at any time in the previous 15 years?
4. Is the action a forestry operation for section 43B purposes?
5. Is there a specific environmental authorisation that instead points to section 43A?
6. Would the action otherwise be likely to have a significant impact on a protected matter under Part 3?

## Evidence to obtain

Useful evidence includes:

- dated aerial imagery;
- historical vegetation maps;
- clearing records;
- development approvals and conditions;
- land-use records;
- any claimed pre-2000 authorisation;
- current clearing plans; and
- evidence identifying whether the relevant vegetation is remnant, regrowth or planted.

Aerial imagery can help establish factual clearing history, but the image itself does not answer the legal question.
