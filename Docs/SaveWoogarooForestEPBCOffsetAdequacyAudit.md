# Save Woogaroo Forest — EPBC 2019/8575 proposed-offset adequacy audit

This note is for Save Woogaroo Forest and assisting/pro-bono lawyers. It is not legal advice. It separates (1) the Commonwealth's own offset-policy tests from (2) public-submission criticisms of the Springview offset proposal and (3) the still-missing primary proponent offset package.

## Bottom line

The public record already identifies a serious offset-law/evidence attack surface, but we should **not yet say the proposed offset has been proved inadequate**.

The Commonwealth's 2012 EPBC Environmental Offsets Policy says, among other things, that offsets:

- compensate only the residual impact left after avoidance and mitigation;
- must correlate to the same protected matter and impacted attribute;
- should generally be as close to the impact site as possible unless a more distant site produces a greater conservation benefit;
- must account for time to ecological gain and confidence/risk;
- must be additional to existing legal/planning/program obligations;
- must be measurable, monitorable, auditable and enforceable;
- must be scientifically robust and precautionary under uncertainty;
- for threatened-species/community habitat, must at least meet impact-site habitat quality or be managed to reach that quality over a defined period;
- must be proportionate in size/scale to the residual impact and the protected matter's status.

A detailed public submission responding to 2019/8575 alleges that the proposed offsets are almost 100 km away, that two are outside the local government area, that regional ecosystem/geology/landscape attributes differ, that one proposed property is in the Mt Walker West/Mt Mort area, that mature hollow-bearing ironbark/ecosystem features cannot be recreated quickly, that MNES baseline/monitoring is inadequate, and that local koala genetics/population structure may not be represented by distant sites.

Those are **submission propositions**, not agency findings. They become legally useful only when tested against the actual proponent offset package and the Commonwealth policy criteria.

## Highest-value tests for counsel/ecologists

### 1. Avoidance and mitigation first

Before asking whether an offset is good enough, identify what impacts remain **after** all feasible avoidance and mitigation.

```text
impact before avoidance
  !=
residual impact eligible for offsetting
```

The primary PD should show alternatives, avoided clearing, retained habitat, mitigation and the final residual impact.

### 2. Same protected matter and same impacted attribute

For each controlled matter, build:

```text
impact attribute
  ->
offset attribute claimed to compensate it
```

Examples include koala foraging/shelter/movement habitat, Grey-headed Flying-fox foraging resources, listed flora habitat, and Lowland Rainforest TEC attributes.

A large number of hectares somewhere else is not itself enough:

```text
large offset area != equivalent ecological function
```

### 3. Distance

The Commonwealth policy does not make distance an automatic invalidator. It says offsets should generally be as close as possible, but a more distant offset can be accepted if a greater conservation benefit is demonstrated.

So the exact counsel question is:

> What greater conservation benefit is said to justify the proposed distant sites, and is that proposition supported for the same protected matter/attribute affected at Woogaroo?

This is stronger than arguing merely that "100 km is too far".

### 4. Habitat quality and ecological time lag

If Woogaroo's impacted habitat includes mature/hollow-bearing or complex long-established forest attributes, the key question is whether the offset starts at equivalent quality or has a credible, resourced trajectory to reach it.

The policy expressly treats time to ecological gain and uncertainty as risk factors.

Therefore:

```text
future revegetation hectares
  !=
present mature-habitat equivalence
```

unless the quality/time/risk calculation actually supports that equivalence.

### 5. Additionality and risk of loss

The offset gain must be additional. Counsel should obtain:

- current tenure;
- existing environmental protections;
- zoning/development constraints;
- any existing conservation covenants;
- existing government/conservation funding;
- baseline probability that the offset vegetation would otherwise be lost;
- the legal instrument proposed to secure the offset.

An area already effectively protected cannot simply be counted again without additional conservation gain.

### 6. Monitoring and success

The public submission questions whether enough baseline MNES surveying exists to establish improvement.

The primary package should therefore identify:

- baseline abundance/use/quality;
- monitoring frequency and method;
- measurable success criteria;
- time horizon;
- confidence assumptions;
- corrective/adaptive actions if targets fail;
- who enforces the obligations;
- whether protection lasts for the life of the impact.

### 7. Local-population and connectivity function

The potentially strongest issue is not generic species substitution but **local functional loss**.

If the Springview habitat contributes to a connected Ipswich koala population/corridor, the question is whether a distant offset improves or maintains viability of the protected matter **in a way that compensates for that lost local attribute**.

Any genetics argument should be independently verified before use. The public submission cites local-population genetic differentiation as a reason distant offsets may not compensate the same population-level function; this remains an acquisition lead, not a proved legal defect.
