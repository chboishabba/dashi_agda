## C. Max-cut, BIDI, Snowball, Pareto and Ibrahim method

**Max-cut:** Cut inference at exact tax base → modelled liability → collected tax → administrative net outcome → appropriation → delivered care → political participation → actual firm voting/control → macro response. Every arrow requires its own data and assumptions. Distinct source types must not be crossed by changing the topic label.

**Bidirectional discriminators:** (i) A liability does **not** guarantee cash collection; an effective enforcement programme *could* still collect it. (ii) A measured migration response does **not** imply severe national collapse; neither does modest aggregate effect mean nobody was negatively affected. (iii) A transfer or forced share sale could reduce some owners' votes; weighted/control instruments can retain a majority. (iv) A property tax can exist in an anti-democratic corporate-sovereign design; such a design does not receive ethical legitimacy from its economic label. (v) Social reproduction and marginal tax rates are separable; they can be causally related, but not identified from a citation alone. (vi) A theoretical distribution tendency is neither logically fate nor a normative right.

**Pareto frontier (no invented proof):** P0 source names, author dates and legal base separation, current finite BIDI regression, topic inventory; P1 exact Agda kernel and CI run, empirical data rights, anti-avoidance validation and fair enforcement; P2 longitudinal beneficial owners ↔ voting instruments ↔ actual transfers ↔ disbursement/outcome; P3 study-specific historical 1942–1964 peacetime causal and cross-jurisdiction tax outcomes; P4 unification with original Wikimedia historical FLN producer and epistemic traversal dataset (distinct project, do not manufacture a 2014 edge).
