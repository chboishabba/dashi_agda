# Save Woogaroo Forest — EPBC 2019/8575 applicable-law matrix

This is a counsel-facing transition-law addendum to `SaveWoogarooForestLegalTeamBrief.md`. It is not legal advice. Its purpose is to stop the pending 1 October 2026 decision from being analysed against the wrong version of the EPBC Act merely because the consolidated Act changed on 24 August 2026.

## Bottom line

EPBC 2019/8575 was referred in 2019 and its assessment approach under s 87 was selected before 24 August 2026. The Environment Protection Reform Act 2025 therefore creates a **mixed applicable-law state**:

```text
pre-24-Aug-2026 assessment framework
  +
selected pre-reform Part 9 approval rules
  +
some post-24-Aug-2026 procedural/post-approval rules
```

There is no valid shortcut from either:

```text
current consolidated Act -> all current provisions apply
```

or:

```text
2019 referral -> entire old Act frozen forever
```

Each provision must be matched to its transition item.

## 1. Assessment on Preliminary Documentation — old framework preserved

Environment Protection Reform Act 2025 Schedule 1 Part 3 item 683(1) says specified amendments do not apply where the Minister decided the assessment approach under EPBC s 87 before commencement.

For 2019/8575 this is directly relevant because the assessment pathway was already Preliminary Documentation before 24 August 2026.

Item 683 specifically preserves, for such an action, the pre-reform position for specified amendments including:

- Division 4 of Part 8 (assessment on Preliminary Documentation);
- s 130(1B);
- specified s 135A amendments;
- the repeal/insertion involving s 136(2)(ba), (c) and (bd);
- other assessment-linked provisions listed in item 683.

So:

```text
pre-commencement s 87 decision
  ->
specified new streamlined-assessment amendments do not apply
```

This does not decide the merits of approval/refusal.

## 2. New ss 136A–136C and substituted ss 138–139 — not automatically applicable

Item 690(1) limits a group of new Part 9 approval provisions to decisions made on or after commencement **where the referral itself was made on or after commencement**.

The list includes:

- specified amendments to s 134;
- new ss 136A, 136B and 136C;
- substituted ss 138 and 139;
- related provisions listed in item 690(1).

Because EPBC 2019/8575 was referred in 2019:

```text
item-690(1) new Part 9 rules
  !=
automatically applicable to 2019/8575
```

This is particularly important for threatened-species analysis: counsel should not quote the substituted post-reform s 139 as though it automatically governs this old referral.

## 3. Some new provisions do apply to old referrals

Item 690(2) expressly applies several inserted provisions to Part 9 decisions made on or after commencement regardless of whether the referral occurred before, on or after commencement. These include the listed new provisions in ss 143–145D.

Item 690(3) separately says s 145AA applies to approvals whether they were given before, on or after commencement.

Therefore:

```text
old referral
  !=
all post-reform law excluded
```

The transition is provision-specific.

## 4. Recommendation-report publication is separately transitional

Item 689 says the amendments to s 135A(2)–(4) concerning publication of recommendation reports apply to a recommendation report given to the Minister on or after commencement, including one prepared before commencement.

This is a useful example of why item 683 cannot be used as a blanket “old law applies” rule.

## 5. Requests for information are another cross-over provision

Item 686 applies specified amendments to EPBC ss 76, 89 and 132 to decisions the Minister is to make whether the underlying action was referred before, on or after commencement.

Counsel should therefore check whether any post-24-August request-for-information mechanism or obligation is available/relevant in the final weeks before the 1 October decision.

## 6. August transitional rules

The Environment Protection Reform (August Commencements) Transitional Rules 2026 commenced on 24 August 2026 and are made under item 715 of Schedule 1 to the Reform Act.

Their operative topics include ministerial declarations/accredited frameworks, bilateral agreements, reconsideration decisions, recovery plans, threat-abatement plans and rulings. The presently identified rules do not appear to replace item 683 or item 690 with a generic rule for old Preliminary Documentation referrals.

That absence should still be verified by counsel against the complete rules and any later instrument before relying on this matrix.

## 7. Practical consequence for the 1 October decision

The legal team should build the federal merits submission using the **operative pre-reform decision provisions actually preserved for this referral**, not the post-24-August substituted provisions merely because they now appear in the consolidated Act.

At the same time, post-commencement provisions that item 689, 690(2), 690(3), 686 or another transition rule expressly applies must not be ignored.

The required counsel product is therefore:

```text
provision
  x
pre-reform text
  x
reform amendment
  x
commencement date
  x
transition item
  x
2019/8575 factual trigger
  ->
operative rule for 1 Oct 2026
```

## 8. Highest-value counsel questions

1. What exact pre-reform text of ss 133, 134, 136, 138 and 139 governs EPBC 2019/8575?
2. Which item-683 preserved Preliminary Documentation rules remain operative through the final decision?
3. Does item 689 affect publication/timing of the recommendation report for this matter?
4. Can the post-commencement ss 76/89/132 information-request amendments still be used before decision?
5. Which item-690(2)/(3) provisions matter if approval is granted, varied or subsequently challenged?
6. Are there any later transitional rules, commencement proclamations or judicial decisions modifying this mapping?
7. Does the transition state create any procedural-review issue if the delegate applies a provision that does not govern this referral?


The important shift is that **transition law is now a bounded, tractable hole**, not an unknown background risk.
