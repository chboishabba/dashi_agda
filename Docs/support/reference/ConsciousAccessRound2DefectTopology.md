# Conscious Access Round Two: Defect, Topology, and Coalition Construction

This tranche advances the network-of-networks formalism from exact toy commutation examples toward a common quantitative spine.

## 1. Typed compositional defect

For a triangle of transforms

```text
A --direct--> C
|             ^
first         | second
v             |
B ------------
```

the finite defect is

```text
direct(x) versus second(first(x)).
```

The implementation distinguishes:

- commuting defect;
- unresolved residual;
- destructive incompatibility;
- productive opposition;
- order residual.

This prevents inhibition or noncommutation from being counted automatically as error. The canonical atlas proves:

```text
total weighted defect = 4
net defect            = 2
```

and a productive extension reduces net defect from `2` to `1`.

## 2. State-mediated effective topology

The anatomical carrier contains sensory-to-association, association-to-planning, and sensory-to-planning edges. Effective openness depends on electrochemical state:

```text
inhibited
permissive
recurrent
```

Under the recurrent state, sensory input reaches planning through association. Under inhibition, the same anatomical edges are not effective. Thus fixed anatomy does not determine current reachability.

## 3. Finite coalition construction

Three complete candidates are evaluated:

```text
local candidate
unstable wide candidate
coherent recurrent candidate
```

The objective combines defect, recruitment cost, reach penalty, and persistence penalty. Their exact objective values are:

```text
local               7
unstable wide       8
coherent recurrent  3
```

The coherent recurrent candidate is proved minimal over the complete candidate type and is a fixed point of the coalition update law. This supplies a genuine finite construction theorem rather than a checklist of desired properties.

## 6. Boundary

The Nat-valued defect and finite objective are exact model quantities, not claimed biological measurements. Minimality does not identify phenomenal consciousness, and a functional edge is not treated as identical to an anatomical edge.
