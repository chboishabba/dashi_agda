## Exact finite theorem A — expected discovery is Schur-concave under both models

Fix **K positive integer type counts** `f=(f_1,...,f_K)`, total `N=Σf_i`. Compare two histograms f and g of equal K and N. Let f majorize g (top-j sorted sums of f are at least those of g, for each j<K).

For a sample of length `0≤t≤N`, the two **different** sampling models are:

**IID with replacement:** each draw has categorical mass `p_i=f_i/N`, and
`E_iid[f,V_t]=Σ_i[1-(1-f_i/N)^t]`.

**Uniform permutation of a fixed finite multiset without replacement:** the multiset has exactly f_i tokens of type i, and
`E_perm[f,V_t]=Σ_i[1 - binom(N-f_i,t)/binom(N,t)]`.

**Theorem, separately for both models:**
`f majorizes g  ⇒  E_iid[f,V_t]≤E_iid[g,V_t]`
and
`f majorizes g  ⇒  E_perm[f,V_t]≤E_perm[g,V_t]`.

These are **expectation** inequalities, not trajectory-by-trajectory comparisons, temporal IID inferences from empirical ranks, or a universal physical scaling theorem.

**Proof by source-owned finite one-token transfer.** For a type count k the expected discovery contribution is a discrete concave function of k. The marginal gains for adding one token of type k, with total N fixed across compared count vectors, are:

`Δ_iid(k;t)=((N-k)/N)^t-((N-k-1)/N)^t`.

For `t>=1`,
`Δ_perm(k;t)=binom(N-k-1,t-1)/binom(N,t)`; for t=0 it is 0.

Both are **nonincreasing in k**. Transferring one token from a smaller type b≥2 to a larger type a≥b changes each total expected discovery by `Δ(a;t)-Δ(b-1;t)≤0`. Finite majorization can be generated by these concentration transfers (or by the standard Hardy–Littlewood–Pólya/Karamata theorem for concave sums). This proves the expected-diversity inequalities for both models.

**Example** for `f=(6,2,1)` versus `g=(3,3,3)`: at t=3,

| Sampler | E for concentrated f | E for dispersed g |
|---|---:|---:|
| IID | 145/81 | 19/9 |
| Uniform fixed-multiset permutation | 40/21 | 16/7 |

The two sampling schemes produce **different** values; their common monotonicity direction does not make their probability spaces identical. At t=N both fixed-multiset samples observe all K types, but IID draws can still miss some types; strict inequality at every t is not asserted.

## Exact finite theorem B — greater Zipf exponent reduces expected IID vocabulary

For a finite shifted Zipf law with fixed support K≥1 and fixed shift q≥0, define

`p_s(r)=(r+q)^(-s)/H_K(s,q)` for nonnegative integer s.

If `s_2≥s_1≥0`, then the likelihood ratio

`p_(s_2)(r)/p_(s_1)(r)=[H_K(s_1,q)/H_K(s_2,q)](r+q)^(-(s_2-s_1))`

is **nonincreasing with rank r**. Since both PMFs sum to one, the positive-to-negative single-crossing of their differences shows `p_(s_2)` majorizes `p_(s_1)`. The same concavity used in theorem A gives, under *separate IID sampling hypotheses*,

`E[V_t | s_2,q,K,IID] ≤ E[V_t | s_1,q,K,IID]`

for every finite integer t≥0. Nothing here proves that an empirical text actually consists of IID draws, that its temporal trajectory follows the expectation, or that a Zipf exponent estimates a physical Sobolev exponent.
