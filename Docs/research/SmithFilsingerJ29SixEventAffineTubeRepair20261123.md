## J28B certificate audit — an important correction

The preceding J28B proof consumer used a signed interval Peano–Baker fundamental matrix evaluated at the **endpoint** of each smooth step. A signed endpoint bound cannot automatically be reused to certify that state errors remain within the same tube **at every intermediate time**: transient error excursions may be larger than endpoint errors because signed terms can cancel by the end of the step. Thus the fourth-event J28B strong certificate required an additional full-time enclosure, despite its exact positive endpoint signs.

J29 repairs this by separating two logically different operations:

1. **Full-time validated flow tube:** For every `0<=s<=h` on a fixed diode/spark branch, a nonnegative interval Jacobian majorant `A>=|Df|`, exact 52nd-order Taylor source-equation residual `epsilon`, and the exact rational bound `exp(Ah)*(delta+h*epsilon)` prove a self-consistent error enclosure for the entire slab. This check is monotone in time and is used for branch-sign, crossing-derivative and tube-inclusion assertions.
2. **Signed endpoint transport:** After full-time inclusion passes, an order-8 **time-ordered, noncommutative** interval Peano–Baker transition `Phi(h)` (with a rational geometric norm tail) transports *only the endpoint* affine uncertainty. The initial nine independent uncertainty symbols remain labelled across every step: `e_k=H_k xi + r_k`, where `|xi_j|<=d_j`; the Jacobian interval matrix multiplies `H` while the residual-forcing component `r` is bounded separately. Additive remainder bounds are rounded **upward** to the existing `10^30` grid, so they never understate errors and do not explode into multi-thousand-digit rational denominators.

This two-track proof fixes interval wrapping without changing the original J24 boxes or selecting a nominal switching time. The J25–J28 consumers using monotone positive flow majorants do not exhibit this *particular* endpoint-versus-all-time flaw. J28B's prior fourth event acceptance is superseded by J29's stronger revalidation; preserve the audit trail rather than silently treating the old check as equivalent.
