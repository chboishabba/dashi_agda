# Save Woogaroo Forest — request for existing Koala monitoring records

## Purpose

This is a plain-English request for **existing records** held by Ipswich City Council or its contractors. It does not ask Council to create a new analysis.

The aim is to obtain local evidence already known to have been collected before commissioning duplicate survey work. The records are relevant to understanding Koala activity, population change, habitat connectivity and fauna-management outcomes around Springfield, Woogaroo Creek, Opossum Creek and White Rock–Spring Mountain.

## Suggested first route: administrative access

Ipswich City Council says administrative access should generally be tried before a formal Right to Information application. Council's public registers also state that monitoring programs carried out under environmental legislation or a development-approval condition, and the results of those programs, can be requested from Planning and Regulatory Services.

Suggested request:

> Please provide electronic copies of existing reports, datasets, maps and monitoring outputs held by Council relating to Koala monitoring and population change in the Springfield, Woogaroo Creek, Opossum Creek and White Rock–Spring Mountain area. We are seeking existing records only and are not asking Council to create a new report or analysis.
>
> In particular, please provide, where held:
>
> 1. the **2020, 2023 and 2025 Ipswich/Biolink Koala monitoring reports and associated data**, including site identifiers, survey dates, survey method, site coordinates or GIS layers, Koala activity/occupancy results and any population-change or metapopulation analysis;
> 2. the underlying results for **White Rock–Spring Mountain Conservation Estate** from those monitoring rounds;
> 3. any **two-yearly detailed analyses of Koala records** undertaken under the Ipswich Koala Conservation and Habitat Management Plan;
> 4. any **biennial Koala activity monitoring** undertaken in Council's Natural Area Estate under that plan;
> 5. monitoring reports, data or maps for **fauna-management solutions where Springfield borders core Koala habitat**, including any annual follow-up reports after implementation;
> 6. any Council-held spatial files or databases used to analyse Koala distribution, habitat use, population configuration or movement in this area;
> 7. any Council-held data-sharing outputs, summaries or de-identified records received from or supplied to the **Ipswich Koala Protection Society, Moggill Koala Rehabilitation Centre**, or other relevant local wildlife-rescue/monitoring organisations for this area;
> 8. any records evaluating fauna movement corridors, exclusion fencing, crossings or other mitigation relevant to Springfield and the White Rock–Spring Mountain interface;
> 9. for each dataset/report, any available metadata explaining coordinate reference system, survey method, detection method, dates, quality-control procedures and limitations.
>
> If some material is held by another Council business unit or by a contractor on Council's behalf, please identify the responsible unit/custodian and, where administratively possible, transfer or route the request to that area.
>
> If any part of this request is considered too broad, please provide the readily identifiable records first and advise how the remainder can be narrowed.

## Why these records are known to exist or be expected

Council's Koala Conservation and Habitat Management Plan describes recurring local monitoring, including detailed analysis of Koala records, biennial monitoring of Koala activity in the Natural Area Estate, and monitoring/reporting of fauna-management measures where Springfield borders core habitat.

Biolink's public project material says:

- a 2020 baseline survey covered Mount Grandchester, Flinders–Goolman and White Rock–Spring Mountain using 63 SAT/Rapid-SAT sites; and
- the 2025 round was the third monitoring round after 2020 and 2023, using 83 permanent Council-estate sites plus 10 private conservation-agreement properties and analysing sightings records alongside field surveys.

Council also publicly describes monitoring-program records and results associated with development conditions as material available by contacting Council.

These sources establish that monitoring programs and reporting pathways exist. They do **not** establish what the unseen results show.

## Evidence boundaries

Please keep these distinctions when using any material received:

- a monitoring **program** is not the same thing as its results;
- a scheduled report is not proof that the report was completed;
- White Rock–Spring Mountain observations are not automatically Springview observations;
- a sighting or rescue record does not by itself define a viable population;
- a structural corridor map does not by itself prove realised Koala movement or gene flow;
- several reports derived from one underlying dataset should not be counted as several independent observations.

The highest-value use of these records is to determine whether existing local evidence can identify the relevant Koala population and current functional connectivity before any new population survey or genetic work is commissioned.
