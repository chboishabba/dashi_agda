# Save Woogaroo Forest — EPBC 2019/8575 operative merits rules

This note is for Save Woogaroo Forest and assisting/pro-bono lawyers. It is not legal advice. It narrows the transition-law problem to the actual Part 9 rules that should be tested against the project evidence.

## Bottom line

For EPBC 2019/8575, the current working position is that the **pre-24-August-2026 merits framework remains the relevant starting point** for the old referral, subject to counsel confirming the Reform Act transition mapping provision-by-provision.

The most important operative provisions are:

- **s 133** — approval/refusal power;
- **s 134** — conditions power;
- **s 136** — mandatory considerations and material to be taken into account;
- **s 139** — threatened-species/community constraints, including approved conservation advice where significant impact is engaged.

Pre-reform **s 138 concerns Ramsar wetlands** and is not presently a controlling-matter rule for 2019/8575 on the source-paid record, which identifies ss 18/18A threatened species and communities as the controlling provisions.

## 1. Section 133 — decision power

The pre-reform s 133 framework allows the Minister, after receiving the assessment documentation, to approve the controlled action for a controlling provision. An approval must identify the action, approval holder, controlling provisions, duration and conditions. A refusal is also an available statutory outcome and must be notified.

This means:

```text
controlled action
  !=
refusal compelled
```

and:

```text
assessment completed
  ->
approval / conditioned approval / refusal remain live outcomes
```

The legal task is therefore to show why the evidence makes refusal, rather than lawful conditioned approval, the correct or legally required outcome.

## 2. Section 134 — conditions are the main counter-case to refusal

Pre-reform s 134 gives a substantial conditions power where the Minister is satisfied a condition is necessary or convenient to protect a Part 3 matter or repair/mitigate damage to it.

That is probably the strongest structural answer the proponent/agency has to a pure refusal argument:

> impacts can be managed through avoidance, mitigation, monitoring, offsets and enforceable conditions rather than refusal.

So the preservation case needs to attack **condition sufficiency**, not merely prove environmental harm.

The key factual questions are:

- which impacts are irreversible before a condition could repair them;
- whether habitat function/connectivity can actually be replaced;
- whether remote offsets protect the same ecological function/population;
- whether avoidance alternatives were genuinely exhausted;
- whether monitoring or fauna-management conditions merely manage harm rather than avoid the protected-matter impact.

## 3. Section 136 — the most important evidence-ingestion rule

Under the pre-reform s 136 framework, the decision-maker must consider matters relevant to each controlling Part 3 provision and economic/social matters, and must take into account the applicable assessment material.

For a Preliminary Documentation assessment, that includes the Preliminary Documentation material and recommendation report, along with other information the Minister has on relevant impacts and relevant comments provided through the statutory processes.

This makes the missing SLQ material especially important. We are not acquiring it merely to improve an advocacy narrative: it is part of the class of material the statutory decision framework itself directs attention to.

The strongest review-oriented question is therefore:

> Did the decision-maker correctly identify and genuinely consider the material evidence on habitat loss, connectivity, fragmentation, alternatives, residual impact and offsets that the operative s 136 framework requires or permits them to consider?

But:

```text
mandatory consideration
  !=
predetermined weight
```

Counsel should distinguish a failure to consider a legally mandatory matter from disagreement about the weight given to evidence.

## 4. Section 139 — threatened species/community constraint

The pre-reform s 139 rule is directly relevant because the source-paid controlling provisions are ss 18/18A.

The pre-reform provision requires the Minister not to act inconsistently with specified international biodiversity obligations or an applicable recovery plan/threat abatement plan. It also requires regard to approved conservation advice where the action has, will have or is likely to have a significant impact on the particular listed threatened species or ecological community.

The immediate evidence work should therefore identify, for each controlling species/community:

```text
species/community
x
approved conservation advice
x
recovery/threat-abatement plan if applicable
x
project habitat/function evidence
x
impact significance
```

This is stronger than citing generic species status because it binds the same project evidence to an operative statutory consumer.

## 5. What this means for the PD extraction

When Part Ai / Part Aii are accessed, extract evidence in this order:

1. exact action and clearing footprint;
2. total, cleared and retained habitat by controlling species/community;
3. habitat-quality / critical-to-survival classifications;
4. observations/surveys and inferred species use;
5. corridor/connectivity and fragmentation analysis;
6. cumulative impacts;
7. alternatives and avoidance;
8. residual impact after mitigation;
9. offsets and conservation areas;
10. references to approved conservation advice, recovery plans and threat-abatement plans;
11. clearing directions, staging and fauna-management measures.

For each item, record:

```text
primary source + exact page/table/map
-> proposition
-> s 133 / 134 / 136 / 139 consumer
-> strongest proponent answer
-> remaining legal/evidentiary hole
```

## 6. Current for/against position

### Strongest preservation side

- multiple threatened species/community controlling provisions are already source-paid;
- the project is in a live Part 9 decision;
- the operative framework requires real consideration of relevant protected-matter evidence;
- s 139 provides species/community-specific legal constraints;
- corridor/fragmentation evidence may be highly material if exact same-object GIS/ecology joins are established.

### Strongest approval side

- controlled-action status does not imply refusal;
- economic/social matters are also part of s 136;
- s 134 provides a broad conditions/mitigation route;
- evidence of species presence or habitat loss alone does not prove that conditions/offsets cannot lawfully manage the impact;
- the final weight and discretion remain with the statutory decision-maker subject to legal limits.

## 7. Counsel questions now narrowed

1. Confirm that the pre-reform ss 133, 134, preserved s 136 components and pre-reform s 139 are the operative merits rules for 2019/8575 after items 683/690.
2. Identify any transition provision that changes that conclusion.
3. Identify which s 136 matters are legally mandatory versus merely permitted and what review consequences attach to omission.
4. For each controlling species/community, identify the applicable approved conservation advice and recovery/threat-abatement instruments.
5. What factual showing would make conditioned approval legally inadequate, rather than merely environmentally undesirable?
6. Can inadequacy of remote offsets to preserve local corridor/population function materially support refusal or a narrower approval footprint?
7. What material can still be placed before the delegate before 1 October 2026, especially under the post-commencement information-request machinery that applies to old referrals?
