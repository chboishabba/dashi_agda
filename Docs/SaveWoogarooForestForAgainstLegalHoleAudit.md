# Save Woogaroo Forest — for/against legal-hole audit

This note is for Save Woogaroo Forest and assisting/pro-bono lawyers. It is not legal advice. Its purpose is to distinguish what the current work has actually closed from what remains evidentially, procedurally or legally open.

## Bottom line

We have **not proved every legal hole for or against preservation**. We have now identified the main legal mechanisms, their key statutory elements, and the strongest obvious counterarguments/defeaters. The remaining work is concentrated in primary evidence, exact procedure, discretion, and project-specific applicability.

In formal terms:

```text
mechanisms identified = yes
statutory tests identified = mostly yes
main defeaters identified = yes
all evidence holes closed = no
refusal legally compelled = not proved
NCA s 13 critical habitat = not proved
s 102 interim order entitlement = not proved
s 49 permanent protection entitlement = not proved
```

## 1. EPBC 2019/8575 — approval/refusal

### For preservation

- Controlled action under ss 18/18A is source-paid.
- Threatened species/ecological-community controlling provisions are source-paid.
- Preliminary Documentation assessment is source-paid.
- Exact delegate and 1 October 2026 decision clock are source-paid.
- EPBC s 136 requires consideration of relevant protected-matter issues and economic/social matters.
- EPBC s 139 requires regard to approved conservation advice where the action has, will have, or is likely to have a significant impact on a listed threatened species/community.

### Against / limiting

- Controlled-action status does **not** imply refusal.
- EPBC Part 9 permits approval with conditions; s 134 is therefore a real alternative to refusal.
- Section 136 expressly includes economic and social matters.
- We have not yet extracted the primary project ecology, impact, avoidance and offset evidence needed to test whether refusal is required, merely open, or weaker than conditioned approval.

### Status

`PARTIALLY CLOSED`

The statutory decision framework is largely mapped. The merits evidence is not yet complete.

## 2. Queensland NCA s 13 — critical habitat

### For preservation

Section 13 defines critical habitat as habitat essential for conservation of a viable population of protected wildlife or a native-wildlife community, and it may include land not presently occupied.

Candidate evidence already identified includes:

- threatened species;
- mature remnant habitat;
- habitat quality;
- official biodiversity-corridor mapping;
- Woogaroo/Opossum connectivity;
- fragmentation;
- regional scarcity;
- movement/dispersal/refuge function.

### Against / limiting

None of these individually proves the statutory test:

```text
species present != critical habitat
mapped koala habitat != critical habitat
federal 'critical habitat' != Queensland s 13
state biodiversity corridor != Queensland s 13
```

The missing proposition is still **same-parcel essentiality to viable-population/community conservation**.

### Status

`PARTIALLY CLOSED`

The legal definition is closed; the factual application is not.

## 3. Queensland NCA s 102 — interim conservation order

### For preservation

Section 102 allows the Minister to make an interim conservation order where threatened/near-threatened wildlife, critical habitat, area of major interest or protected area is subject to a threatening process likely to have significant detrimental effect. Section 103 permits prohibition/control of the threatening process.

Importantly, threatened or near-threatened wildlife is an independent limb; a prior formal s 13 critical-habitat determination is not the only statutory route into s 102.

### Against / limiting

- The power is discretionary: the Minister **may** make the order.
- The Minister must form the required opinion.
- Exact threatening works/timing and likely detrimental effect are not yet same-object paid.
- The order is temporary: s 105 provides no more than 60 days initially, with an extension of no more than 90 days by Governor in Council.

### Status

`PARTIALLY CLOSED`

The statutory route is stronger than our first reconstruction suggested because threatened wildlife itself can trigger the lane, but the evidence and procedure remain open.

## 4. Queensland NCA s 49 — compulsory nature refuge

### For preservation

Section 49 provides a real compulsory route where:

- Minister and landholders cannot agree on a nature-refuge proposal or conservation-agreement terms;
- the Minister considers the area to include an area of major interest or critical habitat;
- the Minister considers that it should be declared a nature refuge.

### Against / limiting

This is not an immediate unilateral campaign remedy. The section requires:

- an antecedent proposal/agreement process and inability to agree;
- ministerial opinion;
- written notice;
- objections;
- eventual Governor in Council declaration by regulation.

It is discretionary and procedurally heavier than s 102.

### Status

`PARTIALLY CLOSED`

The route exists, but we have not proved present entitlement or completed the prerequisites.

## 5. NCA Part 7A / local-government constraint

### For preservation

NCA s 126A prevents a local government from granting an approval inconsistent with a regulation identifying land as, or including, critical habitat or an area of major interest. Section 123 performs a similar function for qualifying conservation plans.

### Against / limiting

This is not triggered by ordinary mapping or by merely arguing that land meets the s 13 definition. The exact land must be identified through the required regulation or conservation plan. Existing approvals/grandfathering may raise separate transitional questions.

### Status

`PARTIALLY CLOSED`

The mechanism is clear. Applicability to the current parcels is open.

## 6. Planning / koala exemption and grandfathering

### For preservation

Council material itself demonstrates that the exemption/history coordinate matters: mapped koala habitat coexists with an approved/exempted outcome.

This creates a potentially valuable attack surface if later amendments, stages, clearing or changed components fall outside the historical exemption.

### Against / limiting

We still do not have the actual primary exemption/grandfathering instrument, dates, legal basis, parcel/stage scope or amendment history.

Without it, we cannot presently prove either:

- that the exemption defeats a current planning argument; or
- that the current action has escaped the exemption.

### Status

`OPEN`

This is one of the largest remaining pure-legal-document holes.

## 7. EPBC s 475 injunction

### For preservation

Section 475 allows the Minister or an interested person to seek a Federal Court injunction against existing or proposed conduct that constitutes an offence or other contravention of the EPBC Act/regulations.

### Against / limiting

- Environmental harm alone is not enough.
- A specific actual/proposed contravention must be identified.
- Lawful conduct under a valid approval and compliant with conditions is not automatically enjoinable simply because it damages habitat.
- Standing, procedure and costs require counsel review.

### Status

`PARTIALLY CLOSED`

The backstop is legally real but presently lacks an identified contravention.

## 8. Corridor / cumulative fragmentation

### For preservation

The official Queensland Statewide Biodiversity Corridors layer plus the campaign project-outline image create a strong hypothesis that Springview/Scenic/Peninsular interact with a broader north-south/riparian connectivity system. This could strengthen:

- EPBC fragmentation/cumulative-impact analysis;
- NCA s 13 essentiality;
- s 102 detrimental-effect evidence.

### Against / limiting

- Screenshots are not an exact GIS join.
- Each project has its own legal/referral identity.
- Corridor status is not itself proof of significant impact or critical habitat.
- A cumulative argument requires a properly sourced bridge between project-specific impacts.

### Status

`OPEN`

Exact polygons + official geometry + expert ecological function are still required.

## What has actually been 'proved'

The work has proved/closed **structural legal questions**, not ultimate outcomes:

1. the relevant statutory routes exist;
2. the main statutory elements are identified;
3. several common WrongType collapses are blocked;
4. the exact federal procedural deadline/delegate is known;
5. the SLQ source for the four-volume PD is known;
6. the major state and federal decision consumers are distinguished;
7. the principal obvious counterarguments/limitations are now explicit.

What is *not* proved is that any decision-maker must refuse, must declare critical habitat, must make an interim order, must create a nature refuge, or that a court would grant relief.

## Current strongest unresolved holes

In order of practical importance:

1. **Primary 2019/8575 PD merits evidence** — habitat loss, retention, quality, avoidance, offsets, corridor/fragmentation analysis.
2. **Exact Queensland s 13 essentiality evidence** — parcel × habitat function × population viability.
3. **Exact clearing/works chronology** for s 102.
4. **Primary Springview exemption/grandfathering instrument and approval history**.
5. **Exact GIS intersection** between project polygons and official corridor/habitat layers.
6. **Any exact offence/contravention** capable of activating EPBC s 475 / Queensland enforcement.
7. **Counsel's view on discretion, procedural vehicle, standing, reviewability and timing**.

## Counsel-facing instruction

The most useful question is now:

> Which of these remaining holes is outcome-determinative, which can be closed before 1 October, and which statutory route has the best combination of legal leverage, evidentiary sufficiency and procedural speed?

That is the point at which pro-bono counsel adds the most value: not discovering generic environmental law, but stress-testing the already-mapped routes against the exact primary evidence and procedural facts.
