\section{Radial-state identity repair and execution receipt}

The historical \emph{DASHI Atom} Hartree code-shaped object accepted subshell
triples $(n,\ell,\mathrm{occ})$, but passed only $\ell$ into its radial
eigenproblem and always requested the lowest eigenpair in that $\ell$ sector.
Consequently $1s$, $2s$, $3s$, \ldots{} collapsed to the same radial target.
This defect is preserved append-only in
\texttt{AtomicPeriodicTable369ArchiveHartreeRadialIdentityDefectExact.agda}; the
later repair does not rewrite the historical source state.

\subsection{Corrected radial selector}

For an analytically controlled hydrogenic central potential, the radial state
index associated with a bound label $(n,\ell)$ is
\[
  r(n,\ell)=n-\ell-1.
\]
The repaired producer
\[
  \texttt{scripts/atomic\_radial\_identity\_repair.py}
\]
therefore diagonalizes each complete $\ell$ sector far enough to contain every
required radial index and selects eigenpair $r(n,\ell)$.  Solving the sector as
one eigenproblem also gives a direct within-$\ell$ orthogonality check rather
than solving the same lowest state repeatedly.

The committed execution receipt is
\[
\texttt{Artifacts/atomic-periodic-table-369/radial-identity-repair-receipt.json}
\]
with producer SHA--256
\[
\texttt{d4df9c2ef1e2292cdf216d7bf5da4aab78189b47cff02dc83bb28d308d240b9e}.
\]
It records Python 3.13.5, NumPy 2.3.5 and SciPy 1.17.0, $4000$ radial grid
points on $r\in(0,80]$ Bohr, and an energy tolerance of $5\times 10^{-4}$
Hartree.

\subsection{First authoritative H I reference-data diagnostic}

The repaired $1s$ energy is consumed by
\texttt{scripts/atomic\_hydrogen\_nist\_comparison.py}.  The committed receipt
uses the 2022 CODATA conversion
\[
  1E_h=27.211386245981\ \mathrm{eV}
\]
and the NIST ASD SRD~78 H I value
\[
  IE_1(\mathrm H)=13.598434599702\ \mathrm{eV}
\]
with quoted uncertainty $1.2\times10^{-11}$ eV.  The finite-grid result gives
\[
  IE_1^{\rm grid}=13.604333505485517\ \mathrm{eV},
\]
so
\[
  IE_1^{\rm grid}-IE_1^{\rm NIST}=+0.005898905783517705\ \mathrm{eV}.
\]
The exact infinite-nuclear-mass Coulomb value represented in the same units is
\[
  IE_1^{\infty M}=13.6056931229905\ \mathrm{eV},
\]
with residual $+0.00725852328850074$ eV.  The finite-grid value is numerically
closer to NIST, but this is not evidence of better physics: discretization error
partially cancels physical-model error.  Both values are retained so that the
cancellation cannot be promoted as accuracy.

\subsection{Leading finite-proton-mass correction}

The next source-paid one-electron correction uses the 2022 CODATA ratio
\[
  \frac{m_e}{m_p}=5.446170214889\times10^{-4}
\]
and therefore
\[
  \frac{\mu}{m_e}=\frac{1}{1+m_e/m_p}=0.9994556794247615.
\]
Applying that factor to the nonrelativistic Coulomb binding gives
\[
  IE_1^{\mu}=13.598287264283275\ \mathrm{eV},
\]
with NIST residual
\[
  IE_1^{\mu}-IE_1^{\rm NIST}=-0.0001473354187240261\ \mathrm{eV}.
\]
Thus the leading finite-nuclear-mass correction reduces the magnitude of the
analytic infinite-mass residual by roughly two orders of magnitude.  It still
does not reach NIST precision. Relativistic, radiative/QED, higher-order recoil,
proton-size and related precision corrections remain explicitly unpaid.

The corresponding producer and receipt are
\[
\texttt{scripts/atomic\_hydrogen\_reduced\_mass\_nist.py}
\]
and
\[
\texttt{Artifacts/atomic-periodic-table-369/hydrogen-reduced-mass-nist-receipt.json}.

\subsection{Many-electron continuation}

The periodic-table-facing next producer has now been authored as
\texttt{scripts/atomic\_helium\_hartree\_scf.py}.  It is a restricted
closed-shell spherical Hartree producer for neutral helium, with two
opposite-spin electrons sharing a $1s$ spatial orbital.  Each electron sees the
Coulomb field of the other electron rather than the full two-electron density,
so the obvious one-electron self-interaction of the archived toy solver is not
reintroduced.  Its intended total-energy bookkeeping is
\[
  E_{\rm He}=2\varepsilon_{1s}-J,
\]
followed by the same-grid ionization difference
\[
  IE_1(\mathrm{He})=E(\mathrm{He}^+)-E(\mathrm{He}).
\]
At this manuscript state the producer is \emph{authored but not yet promoted to
an execution receipt}.  NIST ASD gives the downstream target
$IE_1(\mathrm{He})=24.587389011$ eV with uncertainty $2.5\times10^{-8}$ eV.
Correlation, finite nuclear mass, relativistic/QED effects and generic
multi-orbital state tracking remain unpaid.
