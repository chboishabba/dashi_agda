## Abstract

We develop source-native reductions of the four-dimensional Yang–Mills
mass-gap problem on the original Wilson–Gibbs family. Two sufficient routes
are distinguished: the stronger F1/F3/F4 transfer/recovery formulation and
the direct selected-source covariance-to-Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) spectral
formulation. The latter does not require an independently selected finite
Hamiltonian Mosco limit or a trace-norm transfer theorem. The repository's
R578 critical path organizes the direct route around selected projective
Wilson projections, finite OS applicability, literal CMP119 moment bounds,
original Wilson clustering (WEXT), the transfer coordinate of the
reconstructed Hamiltonian, compact-simple quantitative source data, and a
same-family nontrivial Ward kernel. These are chronological formal
constructions, not claims that all physical inputs are already inhabited.

On the constructive finite/analytic side, we reuse the native Bishop real
and rational SU(2) carriers, canonical centered side-thirteen geometry,
signed perturbation path recursion, and the same dual-carrier compiler for
Bałaban CMP98 equations (119)–(121). We construct root-free local
quaternion logarithm and differential-exponential series using actual
Bishop limit operations, and an explicit reduced inverse on the small
chart. Independent exact rational interval computations verify the local
inverse and logarithm/exponential enclosures, including the removable
zero-radius endpoint. For the constrained Gaussian we distinguish the
original ambient delta elimination from induced subspace volume. An
explicit pivot determinant identity reduces the former to the bordered
operator trace; the latter has a separate Gram determinant correction.
Finite Wilson mixed jets, pivot derivative algebra and quartic
remainder absorption are reused, not reintroduced as new hypotheses.

The remaining local physical source attachments are the selected
variational theorem, geometric and Fréchet identification of the native
chart, the true background derivative of the recovered averaging operator,
the literal CMP109 measure/quadratic form, and a uniform normalized
physical beta enclosure. The later RG and OS consumers must be applied
to the same original family; no inference from a finite configuration
or a source-written proof to full interacting continuum existence is
made. We retain historical sufficient routes and explicit counterexamples
to invalid measure, carrier, and spectral transfers.

The resulting manuscript is a conditional mathematical reduction with
a rapidly developing formal supplement, not an unconditional Clay
completion. The older frozen CMS Drell–Yan comparison remains a bounded

## 2. Literal finite Wilson theory

The finite starting object is the literal four-dimensional SU(2) Wilson/Gibbs family, with gauge-invariant physical Hilbert carrier, normalized vacuum, transfer form, and finite Hamiltonian. In the Lean donor formalization the rescaled energy form is

\[
q_a(\psi,\psi)=a^{-1}\left(\|\psi\|^2-
\operatorname{Re}\langle T\psi,\psi\rangle\right),
\]

where the literal Euclidean transfer operator is

\[
T=P_1^*P_0.
\]

Here `P0` and `P1` are the two slice embeddings of the same Wilson–Gibbs measure. The finite operator is contractive, preserves the vacuum, and the form-to-Hamiltonian infrastructure supplies a self-adjoint finite Hamiltonian with zero-energy vacuum.

There are also fixed-spacing sufficient gap theorems. They are useful non-vacuity checks, but a fixed-spacing strong-coupling condition is not the continuum problem: the physical trajectory simultaneously changes lattice spacing, coupling and volume. The continuum burden is therefore not the existence of *some* finite gap, but a positive physical gap scale that survives the selected `a -> 0`, `beta(a) -> infinity`, volume-growth trajectory.

## 3. F1: the interacting transfer defect

The most economical surviving finite-side physical target is

\[
\exists\Delta>0\;\forall k,n,\psi\perp\Omega_{n,k},
\]

\[
|\langle P_0\psi,P_1\psi\rangle|
\le c_k\|\psi\|^2,
\qquad
\Delta a_k\le 1-c_k.
\]

The second inequality is the important sharpening. The continuum argument does **not** require one constant `c < 1` independent of cutoff. The permitted defect may shrink linearly with lattice spacing. In particular, `c_k -> 1` is compatible with a fixed positive `Delta` provided

```text
1 - c_k >= Delta * a_k.
```

The stronger old uniform-`c` formulation remains sufficient but is not the primitive target.

### 3.1 Three normal forms for F1

The literal transfer analysis exposes three useful ways to pay the same finite-side debt.

#### A. Direct transfer defect

Prove the two-slice bound for every vacuum-orthogonal state and the per-step defect inequality directly. This is the closest form to the final Hamiltonian gap compiler.

#### B. State-uniform truncated correlation

For a vacuum-orthogonal state the disconnected product of one-slice expectations vanishes. Therefore a bound on the connected two-slice correlation supplies the same transfer estimate:

```text
|Corr_c(psi at slice 0, psi at slice 1)|
    <= c_k ||psi||^2
        ->
|<P0 psi, P1 psi>| <= c_k ||psi||^2.
```

This is formalized in `RequestProject/YangMills/Lattice/CorrelationCriterion.lean` as `decorrelation_of_truncated_correlation`.

#### C. Uniform joint-slice density mixing

A stronger sufficient condition is to identify the two-slice joint law relative to the product of its marginals:

\[
\frac{d\nu_{01}}{d(\nu_0\otimes\nu_1)}=1+h_k,
\qquad \|h_k\|_\infty\le \varepsilon_k.
\]

Then every vacuum-orthogonal `L^2` state satisfies the decorrelation estimate with `c_k = eps_k`. This implication is formalized as `decorrelation_of_uniform_joint_density`.

This third form is attractive because it removes operators from the physical input and presents `F1` as a classical mixing statement about the literal Wilson measure. It is also deliberately strong. Polymer convergence or pairwise local-observable clustering cannot be silently relabelled as this full `L^infty` density defect.

### 3.2 Zero coupling and non-vacuity

At zero coupling the two slices factor and the decorrelation constant is zero, uniformly in volume. This establishes that volume growth alone is not the obstruction. The unresolved issue is the interacting continuum trajectory, not the logical consistency of the transfer criterion.

### 5.4 Source-normalized constrained Gaussian determinant

Bałaban CMP109 §2, equations (2.10)–(2.13), first linearizes the nonlinear
fluctuation average and then eliminates the selected pivot coordinates
using the linear delta constraint. The actual remaining covariance is
\((C^\ast AC)^{-1}\). The normalization of the delta and fluctuation
density must be retained, rather than replaced by induced subspace
volume by convention.

For ordinary ambient Lebesgue delta, let \(Q=[P\ R]\) have invertible
\(m\times m\) pivot \(P\), with \(n-m\) free coordinates. Then
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}-P^{-1}R\\I\end{pmatrix},\quad
 \widehat A=C^\ast AC,\quad
 K=\begin{pmatrix}A&Q^\ast\\Q&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
In the coordinates \(x=(u-P^{-1}Rz,z)\), the Jacobian is one and
\(Qx=Pu\). Thus the elementary delta change of variables gives
\[
 Z_\delta=\frac{(2\pi)^{(n-m)/2}}
 {|\det P|\sqrt{\det\widehat A}}.
\]
More generally any additional source density factor must be carried
separately. Congruence of \(K\) by the coordinate change and the identity
on multiplier coordinates replaces \(Q\) by \([P,0]\). Eliminate the
positive free block \(\widehat A\); the remaining saddle block has
determinant \((-1)^m(\det P)^2\), independently of its upper-left block.
This proves
\[
 (-1)^m\det K=(\det P)^2\det\widehat A.
\]
Also \(QQ^\ast=P(I+P^{-1}RR^\ast P^{-*})P^\ast\). Sylvester's
determinant identity gives
\(\det(QQ^\ast)=(\det P)^2\det(C^\ast C)\).
Consequently induced volume and ambient delta differ by
\(\sqrt{\det(QQ^\ast)}\), not by a background-independent scalar in
general.

For \(\Gamma=-\log Z\), at a smooth regular background and two
variation parameters \(s,t\), the ambient-delta result is
\[
 2\Gamma_{st}=\operatorname{Tr}
 (K^{-1}K_{st}-K^{-1}K_tK^{-1}K_s).
\]
The induced-volume result subtracts the same trace expression for
\(M=QQ^\ast\). Equivalently the delta result is the trace expression
for \(\widehat A\) plus **twice** that for \(P\).
This accounts for genuine motion of the eliminated subspace;
tangential changes of free coordinates cancel with the corresponding
coordinate volume. One may use the bordered engine without explicitly
storing \(C'\) or \(C''\), once \(Q,A\) and their actual derivatives are
supplied.

## 5.5. Source-semantic chart and full finite Path13 jet

The squared-chord bound is now attached directly to the rational quaternion path, without a rational operator-norm representation. For positive radii \(a,b\), the identity
\[

gives the weighted triangle bound. The native selected inverse-link radius yields the 61-link gamma and 13-link coarse contour budget \(\delta=37/1024\). The source-written admission uses \(q_0=1-N(q-1)/2\ge1/2\) and \(\|\mathbf q\|^2\le\delta^2\). This is a squared-chord argument, not an equality between a norm and its square.

For a unit quaternion \(q=(a,u)\) in this positive scalar chart, set \(s=u\cdot u\) and \(L(s)=\sum c_n s^n\), with \(c_0=1\) and
\[
c_{n+1}/c_n=(2n+1)^2/((2n+2)(2n+3)).
\]
On \(0\le s\le1/4\), \(0<L\le2\). The geometric derivative tail bound \(\sum_{n\ge N}nr^{n-1}=Nr^{N-1}/(1-r)+r^N/(1-r)^2\), for \(0<r<1\), justifies differentiating on compact subintervals. Put \(F=L+2sL'\). The coefficient recurrence implies \(2(1-s)F'=F\); hence \((1-s)F^2=1\) by its value at zero. Since \(F>0,a>0,a^2=1-s\), one obtains \(aF=1\).

For the root-free stereographic path \(v=u/(1+a)\),
\[
q(t)=((1-t^2|v|^2)/(1+t^2|v|^2),2tv/(1+t^2|v|^2)),\quad0\le t\le1.
\]
The right logarithmic velocity \(q'q^{-1}\) equals \(2v/(1+t^2|v|^2)\). The vector \(y=L(s)u\) lies along a fixed axis along this path, and \(y'=u'/a\) equals the same velocity. Thus \(\exp(-y)q\) is constant, initially one, proving \(\exp(L(s)u)=q\). For arbitrary \(q'=zq\) with \(z\) pure, \(u'=az-u\times z\) and \(s'=2a\,u\cdot z\), giving
\[
D\log=aLI-L[u]_\times+2aL'uu^T.
\]
Writing \(X=2L[u]_\times\), polynomial reduction yields
\(D\log=I-X/2+(aL'/(2L^2))X^2\), using \(aF=1\).
