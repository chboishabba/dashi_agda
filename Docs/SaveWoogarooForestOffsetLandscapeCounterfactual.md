# Save Woogaroo Forest — offset landscape counterfactual

This note is for Save Woogaroo Forest and assisting/pro-bono lawyers. It is not legal advice. It compares publicly source-paid landscape attributes relevant to the EPBC offset `risk of loss`, additionality, habitat quality, connectivity and ecological-time-lag questions while preserving source attribution.

## Attribution discipline

The comparison uses these provenance stages:

```text
primary/external source claim
secondary submission claim
repository reconstruction
cross-source inference
counsel / agency / court conclusion
```

No cross-source inference is attributed back to an external author. No public submission is promoted to an agency finding. No same-region fact is promoted to an exact-offset-parcel fact.

## 1. Woogaroo impact landscape — source-paid official context

Ipswich City Council says Woogaroo Creek and its tributaries flow north from **White Rock–Spring Mountain Conservation Estate** through Springfield, Springfield Lakes, Redbank Plains and Goodna. Council describes the Woogaroo sub-catchment as an **important area for securing urban koala populations** and as part of the **Flinders–Karawatha regional corridor**. It also notes that the Springfield development area lies within this catchment and that the broader SWAG catchment contains some of Ipswich's fastest-growing residential areas.

Separately, Council says **Ric Nattrass Environmental Park** adjoins Woogaroo Creek and provides connectivity for species from White Rock–Spring Mountain Conservation Estate to the Brisbane River corridor.

These are official landscape propositions. They do not by themselves prove that the exact 2019/8575 clearing polygon occupies the decisive connecting segment, but they materially strengthen the acquisition hypothesis.

```text
Woogaroo landscape = urban-development pressure + recognised regional connectivity
```

is source-supported at landscape scale.

## 2. Mt Mort regional landscape — source-paid official context

Ipswich City Council's Koala Conservation Plan describes the **Mt Mort Priority Rehabilitation Area** as a highly rural area. It records:

- State-owned Mt Beau Brummel;
- more than 1,900 hectares of connected land under voluntary conservation agreements and Land for Wildlife;
- a large Queensland Trust for Nature property managed for conservation and koala research;
- a high proportion of the PRA managed for conservation.

Council also says Mt Mort/Hiddenvale has potential to be a koala stronghold.

This gives a real public-source question about **additionality / risk of loss** if a proposed offset parcel is located in that landscape:

> What was the realistic without-offset probability of lawful clearing or habitat loss at the exact parcel?

But this remains a regional question until the exact offset parcel is identified.

```text
Mt Mort regional conservation context
  !=
exact 2019/8575 offset-parcel risk-of-loss value
```

## 3. The public submission lead

A public submission responding to EPBC 2019/8575 identifies a proposed offset property in the **Mt Walker West / Mt Mort** area and criticises differences in geomorphology, vegetation/ecosystem maturity and habitat features.

That is a **secondary submission claim**. It supports acquisition of the exact parcel and proponent response but does not prove the project's final offset location or inadequacy.

## 4. Aroona Station — do not collapse identity

Queensland Trust for Nature publicly says it owns/manages **Aroona Station at Mt Mort**, a working cattle property containing ecosystems supporting koalas. Other public environmental material records conservation and EPBC-offset activity at Aroona Station.

This is relevant context because it shows substantial conservation activity in the Mt Mort landscape.

However:

```text
submission says Mt Walker West / Mt Mort offset
  !=
Aroona Station is the 2019/8575 offset
```

No such identity is presently source-paid.

Do not use Aroona's protection status, lots, vegetation or offset history as if they describe the 2019/8575 parcel unless a primary project source connects them.

## 5. Another Springfield offset is useful only as an analogy

A publicly available **Springfield Rise Additional Offset Management Plan** for **EPBC 2013/7057** describes a Little Kipper Creek Road/Biarra offset about 74 km from that project's additional impact area. It contains non-remnant, regulated regrowth and remnant vegetation, has been affected by pastoral grazing, includes rehabilitation areas, and is adjacent to another protected offset area.

This shows concretely how a Springfield-related EPBC offset can combine:

```text
existing vegetation
+
regrowth
+
non-remnant rehabilitation land
+
future legal protection
```

But it belongs to **EPBC 2013/7057**, not 2019/8575.

Therefore it is only an architecture/comparison example:

```text
other Springfield EPBC offset
  !=
Springview 2019/8575 offset package
```

## 6. Risk-of-loss / development-pressure asymmetry

The Commonwealth offset framework rewards **additional** conservation gain, including avoided future loss. The strongest public-source counterfactual now looks like this:

```text
Woogaroo impact landscape
  urbanising / active development pressure
  recognised corridor position
  existing mature habitat function

versus

candidate offset landscape
  potentially rural
  potentially already conservation-managed
  exact parcel pressure not yet known
```

The repository inference is bounded:

> **If** the exact 2019/8575 offset parcel is already subject to substantial conservation protection or low realistic clearing pressure, then its avoided-loss credit may be materially smaller than raw hectares imply.

That is a DASHI cross-source inference, not a claim made by Ipswich City Council or QTFN.

## 7. Existing mature forest versus planted/regrowth habitat

The comparison must be attribute-by-attribute, not hectare-for-hectare.

For the impact site, establish:

- remnant/regrowth status;
- dominant tree species;
- tree-size/age distribution;
- canopy continuity;
- mature/hollow-bearing trees where relevant;
- fallen timber and structural complexity;
- actual koala/flying-fox/listed-species use;
- movement/corridor role.

For each offset parcel, establish the same coordinates plus:

- how much is existing remnant habitat;
- how much is regrowth;
- how much is bare/non-remnant/planted restoration;
- starting habitat-quality score;
- future-with-offset score;
- years until the claimed future score;
- confidence of achieving it.

The legal/evidence firewall is:

```text
same hectares
  !=
same present habitat function
```

and:

```text
future tree cover
  !=
present mature-forest equivalence
```

without an explicit quality/time/confidence bridge.

## 8. Protected-area proximity has two different effects

Proximity to existing protected land can increase ecological value by extending or joining a secured network. But it can simultaneously reduce **additionality** if the proposed offset land is already effectively protected or faces little realistic loss.

So counsel/ecologists should keep two questions separate:

```text
Does protected-area adjacency increase ecological connectivity value?
```

and:

```text
Does existing protection / low development pressure reduce the new avoided-loss gain?
```

Neither answer determines the other.

## 10. Current strongest counsel questions

1. **Risk of loss:** what evidence supports the proponent's without-offset clearing probability for each proposed offset parcel?
2. **Additionality:** how much of the proposed conservation gain would occur anyway because of zoning, vegetation law, conservation agreements, public ownership or existing conservation management?
3. **Maturity:** what ecological attributes of the impacted mature forest exist at the offset site on day one rather than being promised decades later?
4. **Connectivity:** does the offset replace the loss of an urban corridor function connecting secured habitat, or merely protect habitat elsewhere?
5. **Protected-area adjacency:** is adjacency being counted as conservation value while pre-existing protection is ignored in the additionality baseline?
6. **Species/population:** does the offset benefit the same protected matter at the relevant population/function scale?
