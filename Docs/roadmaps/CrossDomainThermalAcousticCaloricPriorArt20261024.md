## Correct consumer-first comparison

The common *service* must be declared before ranking technologies:
- service: delivered heating power or cooling duty, cold/hot endpoint temperatures and ΔT, load profile, inlet/return temperatures, humidity and refrigeration safety;
- actual delivered performance: measured COP **at plug/wall**, capacity under load at same temperature lift, energy to circulate pumping fluid, field/motor/mechanical/compressor work, parasitic heat leaks and waste rejection;
- source technology: input thermodynamic driving grade, reservoir exergy, field/stress/electric/magnetic/pressure actuation;
- robustness: thermal cycling, fatigue, magnetocaloric corrosion, leak/refrigerant/working fluid integrity, maintenance/repair and part-load behaviour;
- externalities: acoustic spectra, vibration and human receptor dose separately, water, refrigerant release, carbon of actual displaced heating baseline, lifecycle materials/energy and costs;
- metrology: same system/site/batch/duty/time, calibrated sensors, uncertainty, original primary source, full loss chain;
- maturity: theoretical bound != material experiment != standalone prototype != measured loaded system != multi-unit demonstration != commercial fleet.

Rankings must be Pareto-/service-specific, no universal "best cooler". For real commercial/industrial heating TODAY, benchmark ordinary heat exchangers and incumbent heat pumps as default comparators; caloric/thermoacoustic alternatives compete only where measured duty and physical constraints fit. Temperature span alone never equals refrigeration capacity.
