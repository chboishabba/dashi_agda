## Original theoretical authors and contribution boundary

The speculative film triangle in the user-supplied prompt is an
**analytical diagram**, not automatically the same mathematical object
as Peirce's sign/object/interpretant triad. A character in the diegesis
is not always the Peircean object, and a spectator is not definitionally
identical to an interpretant. A translation requires selected
source-specific work and explicit maps.

Further literature to cite with independent original-passage review:
- Charles Sanders Peirce, triadic semeiotic (original sign/object/
  interpretant works); NOT owner of DASHI's mathematical non-descent.
- Roland Barthes, "The Third Meaning" (1970): obtuse filmic signification.
- Laura Mulvey, "Visual Pleasure and Narrative Cinema" (1975):
  feminist analysis of cinematic gaze.
- Jean-Louis Baudry and Christian Metz: cinematic apparatus and
  identification, with their theories kept distinct.
- Gilles Deleuze, `Cinema 1` and `Cinema 2`: movement/time-image.
- Vivian Sobchack, `The Address of the Eye` (1992): phenomenology.
- Murray Smith, `Engaging Characters` (1995): recognition,
  alignment and allegiance; not reducible to Lacanian identification.
- Noël Carroll, `The Philosophy of Horror` (1990), and
  David Bordwell, `Narration in the Fiction Film` (1985).
- Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, `Touching Feeling` (2003): reparative
  and paranoid reading, without flattening these into political status.
- Foucault/Derrida/Lacan/Žižek: consume existing source-bounded owners.

**Attribution rule:** prior mathematical theorems remain credited to
their DASHI owners; the cited thinkers own their intellectual arguments;
the film's credited creators own their works. This application does not
claim conceptual authorship, consent, endorsement or historical
psychoanalysis by any original creator.

## `Léon` adversarial scene case

Work/edition/scene must be selected from original footage or a
reviewed cut-specific transcript. Character refusal, milk-spitting
comic effect and cinematographic framing are independent
claim-bearing channels; the student's interpretive response is neither
evidence of every audience member's response nor a diagnosis of
a performer or director. Cut/version alignment matters.

The proposed claim that "the film leers" is a contested formal critical
interpretation to ground in specific camera positions and editing,
not itself an independently measured viewer effect. Likewise,
reassurance at the refusal is an admissible personal reception report,
not proof of universal authorial intention.
