# Save Woogaroo Forest — Koala population and connectivity expert brief

## Purpose

This note is for an independent ecologist, koala researcher or conservation geneticist. It asks for a focused opinion on the two live Queensland legal questions:

1. whether the approved Springview clearing/earthworks process is likely to have a significant detrimental effect on threatened wildlife for the Nature Conservation Act ss 102–103 route; and
2. whether the relevant Springview/Woogaroo habitat is essential to conservation of a viable Koala population/community for the s 13 route.

It is not asking the expert to decide the law. It is asking for the ecological facts and causal reasoning counsel needs.

## What is already established from project/local sources

The project’s own 2019 ecology records Koala scats, recognised food trees, habitat score 7/10, a connected-habitat surface greater than 500 ha, approximately 136 ha direct Koala-habitat clearing and approximately 26 ha indirect impact. The same consultant concluded that clearing and functional loss of about 136 ha would significantly affect Koala habitat critical to survival under the federal guideline then used.

Ipswich City Council independently describes the Woogaroo Creek sub-catchment, including Opossum Creek, as an **important area for securing urban koala populations** and as part of the **Flinders–Karawatha regional corridor**.

Council’s Koala Conservation Plan describes the White Rock–Spring Mountain conservation estate as part of that bioregional corridor and says the corridor allows fauna movement, migration and transfer of genetic diversity. It also identifies urban development from Springfield along the eastern/north-eastern boundary as an important future threat context.

Queensland’s SEQ koala mapping program defines Koala Priority Areas as large connected areas with the highest likelihood of sustaining SEQ koala populations in the long term. That is useful official spatial methodology, but it is not itself proof that the Springview parcel satisfies the NCA s 13 definition.

## What the independent literature adds

The selected literature does not prove the Springview case. It identifies the variables that a defensible local analysis should address.

- McAlpine et al. (2006), DOI `10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01603.x`: SEQ Koala presence was best explained by a multilevel model including the amount of high-quality habitat, neighbourhood effects, spacing/density of forest patches and sealed-road density.
- Rhodes et al. (2006), DOI `10.1016/j.biocon.2006.03.021`: separates forest area/configuration effects from local habitat factors in a Queensland Koala landscape.
- Lee et al. (2010), DOI `10.1007/s10592-009-9987-9`: identifies genetic structuring in SEQ Koalas and barriers consistent with roads, rivers and urbanisation.
- Dudaniec et al. (2013), DOI `10.1111/mec.12359`: provides primary SEQ landscape-genetic evidence linking landscape structure to Koala genetic connectivity.
- McLennan et al. (2025), DOI `10.1002/eap.3062`: identifies genomic erosion in several Koala populations, including coastal southeast Queensland, and treats highways/cities as important barriers to dispersal.
- Brunton et al. (2026), DOI `10.1002/2688-8319.70253`: current systematic review of Koala connectivity mapping. It is useful particularly because it cautions against treating structural corridor maps as realised functional connectivity without validation.

## Questions for the expert

Please address these separately and state uncertainty where the data do not allow a firm answer.

### A. Identify the relevant population/community

- What is the biologically defensible Koala population or population network relevant to Springview/Woogaroo/Opossum Creek?
- What evidence supports that boundary: telemetry, genetics, repeated observations, density, rescue/clinical records, movement barriers, roads, waterways or other monitoring?
- Is the development boundary biologically arbitrary relative to that population?

### B. Test functional connectivity

- Is the Woogaroo/Opossum/Springview landscape merely structurally connected on a map, or is there evidence that Koalas actually move through or depend on it?
- Does the Springview habitat function as a bottleneck, stepping-stone, refuge, dispersal route or ordinary replaceable patch?
- How do roads, existing development and retained creek/open-space strips affect realised movement and gene flow?

### C. Run the with-site / without-site counterfactual

Compare the current landscape with a plausible future in which the approved clearing/earthworks proceed.

Please address likely effects on:

- local persistence;
- movement/dispersal;
- breeding access and recruitment;
- access to food/shelter resources;
- gene flow / isolation risk;
- road exposure and other indirect mortality;
- resilience to further surrounding development;
- time required for any proposed restoration/offset habitat to perform the same function.

### D. Address the strongest contrary evidence

Saunders Havill assigned the site a Koala recovery value of 0 and argued that roads/urbanisation make it relatively isolated and not viable to support a Koala population.

Please explain whether that conclusion remains ecologically persuasive in light of:

- the same report’s greater-than-500-ha connectivity finding;
- Council’s later description of the Woogaroo/Opossum area as important for securing urban Koala populations;
- the Flinders–Karawatha corridor context;
- subsequent development/fragmentation pressure;
- current observations or monitoring data, if available.

### E. Apply the s 102 ecological question

Without giving a legal opinion, please state whether, in ecological terms, the approved vegetation clearing/earthworks process is **likely to cause a significant detrimental effect** on the relevant threatened Koala population/habitat after taking the actual mitigation measures into account.

Please identify:

- causal pathway;
- likelihood;
- magnitude;
- duration;
- reversibility;
- cumulative/fragmentation effects;
- mitigation effectiveness and uncertainty.

## What would most improve the opinion

The highest-value additional evidence would be:

1. current local telemetry, genetics, density or repeated-monitoring data;
2. current habitat condition and recent clearing/development change;
3. local road-strike/rescue/clinical records;
4. evidence of use of Opossum/Woogaroo creek corridors;
5. performance evidence for retained strips/crossings/fauna controls, not just their existence;
6. the substantive 2026 EPBC Preliminary Documentation and response to public comments;
7. current 9281 Condition 6(a), prestart and commencement records.
