# Save Woogaroo Forest — Queensland interim conservation order issue note

## What this note is for

This note identifies the practical legal question under **Part 6 of the Nature Conservation Act 1992 (Qld), especially ss 102–107**. It is not a court pleading and does not assume that an order will be made.

## The statutory question

Section 102 allows the Minister to make an interim conservation order if the Minister is of the opinion that one of the following is subject to a threatening process likely to have a significant detrimental effect:

- threatened or near-threatened wildlife;
- protected wildlife habitat that the Minister considers to be critical habitat;
- an area of major interest; or
- a protected area.

Section 103 allows an order to prohibit or control the threatening process. It also expressly allows an order to relate to land even if the wildlife or habitat is not physically within that land.

The legislation therefore does **not** require perfect map overlap between a clearing polygon and a wildlife observation.

## What still matters most

The central question is not whether every ecological point lies inside an exact clearing boundary. It is:

**Is the approved clearing/earthworks process capable of affecting qualifying wildlife, habitat or an area in a way that is likely to have a significant detrimental effect?**

The most useful remaining evidence is therefore:

1. the present status of Condition 6(a);
2. whether a prestart meeting has occurred;
3. whether clearing or earthworks are imminent or have commenced;
4. current ecological evidence relevant to likely effect;
5. any expert opinion needed to connect the approved process to likely significant detrimental effect; and
6. the substantive 2026 EPBC Preliminary Documentation, because it may contain the most current impact, mitigation and offset analysis.

## Important legal cautions

- `9281/2024/OW` being approved does not prove works have started.
- Absence of prestart or compliance documents from a public Development.i page does not prove that those records do not exist.
- The existence of habitat impact does not automatically mean the s 102 test is satisfied.
- A prior s 13 critical-habitat declaration is not necessarily required for every s 102 pathway, because s 102 separately refers to threatened or near-threatened wildlife.
- Section 102 creates a Ministerial interim-conservation-order mechanism. It should not be described as an “s 102 injunction”. Separate court injunction or judicial-review routes need their own legal basis.
- If an interim conservation order is made, s 107 provides a mechanism by which an authority under another Act may be suspended to the extent it permits conduct that would contravene the order.

## Questions for counsel

1. Which s 102 gateway is strongest on the present evidence: threatened wildlife, critical habitat, area of major interest, or another route?
2. What level and type of evidence is usually needed to support the Ministerial opinion that the threatening process is likely to have a significant detrimental effect?
3. Can an approved but not yet commenced clearing process support an urgent request where commencement is imminent?
4. What evidence should be obtained immediately from Council, the proponent or the Commonwealth to establish the present execution state?
5. If an interim conservation order were made, what practical effect could s 107 have on existing local approvals or authorities?
6. What separate judicial-review, injunction or enforcement options should be preserved if the administrative route is not available or is too slow?
