# Save Woogaroo Forest — offset landscape, maturity and counterfactual-risk addendum

This addendum is for Save Woogaroo Forest and assisting/pro-bono lawyers. It is not legal advice. It extends the EPBC 2019/8575 offset audit using official public material available without the SLQ Preliminary Documentation volumes.

## Why this matters

The useful offset comparison is not simply:

```text
hectares cleared at Woogaroo
vs
hectares protected/planted elsewhere
```

It is closer to:

```text
impact-site ecological function now
x protected matter
x landscape position
x development pressure
x maturity / vegetation condition

versus

offset-site ecological function
x additional gain over its no-offset future
x time to equivalent function
x confidence of success
x legal security
```

The Commonwealth Offset Assessment Guide expressly treats habitat quality, risk of loss, time until ecological benefit and confidence as inputs. This makes development pressure, existing protection and forest maturity legally/evidentially relevant to the adequacy analysis.

## 1. Development pressure / risk of loss

The Commonwealth defines `risk of loss` as the chance that habitat at an offset site would otherwise be permanently lost in the foreseeable future.

That creates an important counterfactual question:

> What would likely happen to the offset parcel if it were **not** made an offset?

A parcel exposed to genuine lawful clearing/development pressure may generate meaningful avoided-loss gain when secured. A remote parcel already unlikely to be cleared, or already strongly constrained/protected, may generate substantially less additional gain from another protection instrument.

Therefore:

```text
large offset area
  !=
large additional conservation gain
```

For each offset site, counsel/ecologists should compare:

- zoning and development designation;
- nearby approved/proposed development;
- road/infrastructure pressure;
- historical clearing;
- active development applications;
- tenure;
- existing statutory clearing constraints;
- existing conservation covenant/reserve status;
- realistic lawful development pathway.

No numeric probability should be asserted without a defensible planning/economic model. The immediate legal question is whether the proponent's assumed `risk of loss` is evidence-based.

## 2. Woogaroo's protected-area/network context

Official Ipswich material identifies White Rock–Spring Mountain Conservation Estate as more than 2,500 hectares and as a core habitat area within the Flinders–Karawatha Corridor. Council says the estate has very high natural/conservation values and forms the headwaters of seven waterways.

Council also identifies Ric Nattrass Environmental Park on Woogaroo Creek as providing connectivity for species from White Rock–Spring Mountain to the Brisbane River corridor.

The historic Springfield Structure Plan is particularly important: it says Spring Mountain–White Rock and Greenbank are crucial core habitat areas and that Springfield's role is to provide a habitat connection between them; it also identifies protection of the upper Woogaroo Creek catchment and a linkage to the White Rock/Spring Mountain conservation area.

This does not yet prove the exact 2019/8575 polygon is the indispensable connecting segment. It does establish that **connectivity through Springfield and Woogaroo Creek is an official planning/conservation concept**, not merely campaign language.

The exact next spatial test is:

```text
Springview action polygon
x White Rock–Spring Mountain / Woogaroo Creek network geometry
x official corridor geometry
x species use
```

## 3. Mature existing forest versus planted/regrowth offset

Commonwealth offset guidance evaluates present habitat quality, future quality and `time until ecological benefit`. Queensland ecological guidance distinguishes mature good-quality regrowth from degraded regrowth and new revegetation because successional stage changes ecological value, management effort and failure risk.

Existing mature forest can immediately contain functions that planting does not instantly recreate, including:

- mature food/shelter trees;
- canopy continuity;
- established floristic composition;
- understorey complexity;
- standing dead trees, hollows and fallen timber where relevant;
- soil/ecosystem structure;
- established movement pathways;
- long-standing species use.

Queensland wildlife guidance explicitly notes that some animals require trees and shrubs of particular size or age and separately identifies hollows and fallen timber as wildlife habitat features.

Hence:

```text
planted tree cover
  !=
mature forest ecological function
```

and:

```text
young regrowth hectares
  !=
old/mature habitat hectares
```

unless a protected-matter-specific quality/time/confidence bridge is demonstrated.

For every impact and offset site obtain, where possible:

```text
remnant / regrowth / planted status
successional stage / estimated stand age
canopy structure
mature-tree density
food-tree composition
hollows / dead standing trees / fallen timber
understorey / floristic composition
actual species-use evidence
connectivity role
time to equivalent ecological function
confidence in reaching that state
```

## 4. Species-specific comparison

The offset comparison should be run separately for each controlled matter rather than assuming one generic 'forest' metric pays all species.

For koala, official Queensland material emphasises suitable food/shelter vegetation, sightings, connected priority areas and long-term population persistence. Queensland also identifies clearing and fragmentation of eucalypt forests/woodlands as drivers of local population decline.

For Grey-headed Flying-fox and listed birds, flowering/fruiting resource composition, seasonality and landscape movement may matter differently.

For listed flora and the Lowland Rainforest ecological community, floristic composition, geology/soil, regional ecosystem identity, condition and successional state may be more important than simply tree density.

Therefore:

```text
same broad vegetation label
  !=
same protected-matter attribute
```

## 5. Proximity to protected land cuts both ways

Adjacency to an existing protected estate can increase ecological value by enlarging/connecting habitat. But if the offset parcel is already effectively protected or has low development pressure, the additional avoided-loss gain may be smaller.

Those are separate coordinates:

```text
protected-area adjacency
  -> potentially higher connectivity/ecological value
```

while:

```text
pre-existing protection / low loss risk
  -> potentially lower additionality credit
```

Counsel should require both to be shown rather than accepting adjacency as automatically beneficial for the offset calculation.

## 6. Public-source work possible now

Without the SLQ volumes, the highest-value work is:

1. obtain exact Springview/Scenic/Peninsular polygons;
2. map distance and connection to White Rock–Spring Mountain, Woogaroo Creek/Ric Nattrass and official biodiversity corridors;
3. intersect the project land with official remnant/regrowth and koala-habitat mapping;
4. identify the publicly described offset parcels with exact lot/plan/coordinates where possible;
5. perform the same protected-area, vegetation-condition and development-pressure audit on those sites;
6. compare like-for-like protected-matter attributes;
7. reserve all claims about the proponent's actual calculator inputs until primary material is obtained.

## 7. Counsel-facing question

The strongest current offset question is:

> **Does the proposed offset package genuinely compensate the immediate loss of mature, connected habitat in an urban-pressure landscape, or does it principally secure or plant different/lower-risk habitat elsewhere whose apparent hectare gain overstates the additional, timely and functionally equivalent conservation benefit?**

That question is deliberately neutral about the answer. It tells counsel exactly what to test on both sides.
