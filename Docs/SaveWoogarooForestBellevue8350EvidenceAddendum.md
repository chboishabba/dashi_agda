# Save Woogaroo Forest — Bellevue Woods / EPBC 2018/8350 evidence addendum

This addendum is for Save Woogaroo Forest and assisting/pro-bono counsel. It records project-specific primary evidence from the EPBC 2018/8350 Eugene Street / Bellevue Woods referral package. It must not be silently transferred to EPBC 2019/8575 or another project.

## Why 2018/8350 matters to the wider Woogaroo case

The 2018/8350 referral package gives unusually useful proponent-side evidence about the northern Woogaroo Creek landscape:

- the project site was about 33.8 ha;
- the proposed disturbance footprint was about 22.9 ha;
- about 21.2 ha of regulated/native vegetation was proposed to be cleared;
- Woogaroo Creek and its riparian corridor were proposed to be retained/restored;
- the proponent concluded the action was likely to have a significant impact on koala;
- approximately 21.3 ha of native vegetation scored 6 under the Koala Habitat Assessment Tool, which the referral characterised as habitat critical to koala survival;
- the proponent described Woogaroo Creek as connective to larger remnant tracts to the south and to Brisbane River riparian habitat;
- the proponent referred to White Rock, Spring Mountain and Greenbank as larger surrounding habitat tracts.

These are 2018/8350 project facts / proponent assessments. They are not agency findings for Springview.

## Existing habitat versus restoration / planting

The referral's own design history is directly relevant to the current offset-quality question. It says that retaining remnant vegetation would help reduce the lag in time while ecological restoration establishes, and that staged clearing could retain habitat for as long as possible while restoration works establish.

The koala environmental-outcomes table then uses:

- medium-term milestones of about 10 years for evidence of koala use of the corridor/enhancement areas; and
- long-term goals of 20+ years for achievement of a layered forest providing high-quality koala habitat.

Accordingly, within the same Woogaroo Creek landscape the proponent itself distinguishes:

```text
existing retained remnant habitat
    !=
young restoration / newly planted habitat
```

in temporal and ecological-function terms.

This does not automatically invalidate any Springview offset. It is, however, strong comparative evidence for asking whether an offset calculator properly accounts for present habitat function, maturity, time to ecological benefit and confidence in achieving equivalent function.

## Corridor and cumulative-impact relevance

The 2018/8350 referral states that:

```text
Woogaroo Creek remains connective to larger remnant vegetation to the south
and to Brisbane River riparian habitat.
```

This matters to the wider project chain because Bellevue Woods sits at the northern end of the campaign's Springview–Scenic–Peninsular corridor framing.

The correct cross-project question is therefore:

> Do the later projects cumulatively narrow, sever or degrade the same Woogaroo Creek / remnant-mosaic connectivity that the earlier 2018/8350 proponent itself recognised?

This remains a cross-source hypothesis until exact project polygons and corridor geometry are joined.

## Koala population / site-use relevance

Attachment 17 states that the site supported recent koala evidence and cites 71 Wildlife Online records within a 1 km radius, together with survey evidence and nearby Ric Nattrass Environmental Park and White Rock–Spring Mountain Conservation Estate as notable koala locations.

That makes Bellevue a strong source-paid northern landscape datum for a later population/corridor model, but not a substitute for current Springview survey evidence.

## Gain/loss design trade-off

The 2018/8350 referral is also useful for the Snowball gain/loss analysis. It records:

- an initial maximum-yield concept of 395 lots;
- a later design option losing 71 lots to larger linear open-space/buffer arrangements;
- another option losing 63 lots;
- further refinement to retain more significant vegetation;
- engineering and stormwater requirements as an ultimate driver of later road/stormwater layout.

That proves a real project-design trade-off between development yield, infrastructure constraints and ecological retention.

It does **not** prove improper motive or wrongdoing.

## Highest-value use in the current handoff

The 2018/8350 material should now be used for three bounded purposes:

1. **corridor continuity:** primary evidence that Woogaroo Creek was already understood by a proponent as a connective north-south ecological feature;
2. **maturity/time lag:** primary same-landscape evidence that retained remnant habitat and restored/planted habitat are temporally and functionally distinct;
3. **cumulative fragmentation:** a baseline against which Springview, Scenic and Peninsular should be assessed as later interventions in the same broader corridor system.

The next exact step is to spatially weld the 2018/8350 site polygon, current Springview/Scenic/Peninsular polygons and the official Queensland corridor geometry.
