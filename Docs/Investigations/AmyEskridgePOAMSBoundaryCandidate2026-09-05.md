# Amy Eskridge 2020 NASA-review paper — POAMS candidate identity pass

## Question

Does the unnamed paper Amy Eskridge said in September 2020 was under NASA review correspond to the later public NASA Technical Memorandum:

> Richard H. Eskridge, Michael A. Nelson, Michael P. Schoenfeld, *A Study of the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis Theory (POAMS) Including a Mathematical Reformulation and Validation Experiment*, NASA/TM–20205010911, report M-1531?

## Earlier object

A captured September 2020 statement attributed to Amy says:

- the theory/data she intended to present depended on novel foundational work generated by a member of their team while a NASA MSFC civil servant;
- the work was subsequently matured privately by The Institute;
- the NASA-origin foundational work had to receive NASA approval for public release before subsequent results could be discussed publicly;
- a paper was under NASA review for publication and was expected soon.

The captured statement does not provide a paper title, NASA identifier, author list, review number or final outcome.

## Later public candidate

NASA NTRS record 20205010911 establishes:

- title: *A Study of the Pope-Osborne Angular Momentum Synthesis Theory (POAMS) Including a Mathematical Reformulation and Validation Experiment*;
- authors: R. H. Eskridge, M. A. Nelson, M. P. Schoenfeld;
- acquisition source: Marshall Space Flight Center;
- date acquired: 1 December 2020;
- publication date: 1 November 2021;
- report number: M-1531;
- distribution limits: Public;
- copyright: Work of the US Government, Public Use Permitted.

This is temporally and institutionally compatible with Amy's September 2020 statement.

## NASA process context

NASA's STI policy independently confirms that NASA STI intended for external dissemination requires release authorization through the NF-1676/DAA process or equivalent review. The review can assess whether information has prohibited or restricted public access.

This supports the procedural plausibility of Amy's statement that NASA-origin STI required release approval.

It does **not** establish that:

- the material was classified;
- NASA suppressed the work;
- the release was denied;
- Amy's paper was necessarily the POAMS TM;
- the technical claims were correct.
