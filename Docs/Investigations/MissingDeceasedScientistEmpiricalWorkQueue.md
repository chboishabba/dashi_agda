## Tier 4 — real common-observer surfaces

Search public/archival evidence first:

- award-management and programme-review systems;
- grants, contracts, task orders, SBIR/STTR records;
- shared prime/subcontractor and supplier networks;
- advisory panels, workshops, review boards and standards bodies;
- facility-access or laboratory-user systems where lawfully available;
- professional-society/conference networks;
- declassified/official technical-intelligence or counterintelligence records only where a provenance-bearing source exists.

Deliverable: named observer/system with same-object receipts for multiple roster members.

## Tier 5 — actor-specific disruption benefit

Only after Tiers 1–4 produce a discriminating pattern, test specific actor classes for:

- material economic or strategic exposure to the affected capability;
- contemporaneous evidence that the actor perceived the capability as a threat or competitive problem;
- person-specific visibility or access;
- measurable gain from programme delay or capability degradation.

Examples of hypotheses to test, not presume:

- incumbent energy interests materially exposed to commercial fusion/fission substitution;
- foreign strategic competitors benefiting from US capability delay;
- rival programmes/contractors benefiting from funding or contract shifts.

Deliverable: source-backed benefit/visibility pair, still separate from perpetration.

## Tier 6 — case-specific attribution evidence

Required before any serious actor-attribution claim:

- authenticated communications;
- travel/access records;
- financial transfers;
- surveillance or contact evidence;
- forensic linkage;
- operational records;
- independent witness evidence;
- judicial, congressional, inspector-general, FOIA/declassified or equivalent authenticated records.

Historical precedents such as Stuxnet remain **capability/mode-of-action analogues only** unless a case-specific bridge is found.

## Current stopping condition

If matched-control analysis shows no meaningful enrichment and post-loss impact is ordinary relative to peers, downgrade the corresponding selection hypothesis rather than expanding it.

If multiple technical narratives fail while the roster remains heterogeneous, elevate retrospective assembly / unrelated-case hypotheses.

If one feature survives controls, predicts unusual disruption and maps to a real discriminating observer surface, then focus acquisition on that feature and stop broadening the search.
