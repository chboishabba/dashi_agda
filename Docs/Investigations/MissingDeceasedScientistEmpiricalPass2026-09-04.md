# Missing/deceased scientist investigation — empirical pass 2026-09-04

This is an evidence-gathering pass, not a causal theory. It records only what the located sources currently support and what they fail to support.

## 1. Nuno F. G. Loureiro

### Event chronology
- MIT states Loureiro died on 16 December 2025 from gunshot wounds sustained hours earlier.
- MIT later stated that authorities had identified the individual responsible for Loureiro's killing and for the Brown University mass shooting.

Sources:
- MIT Office of the President / MIT News, 16 Dec 2025: https://orgchart.mit.edu/letters/professor-nuno-loureiro-1977-2025
- MIT News statement, 19 Dec 2025: https://news.mit.edu/2025/statement-professor-nuno-loureiro

### Post-loss disruption
- By March 2026, MIT identifies Stephen J. Wukitch as interim director of the Plasma Science and Fusion Center (PSFC).
- The PSFC remained publicly active in 2026, including research and congressional-facing technology activity.
- This is evidence of leadership continuity at the centre level. It is not evidence that Loureiro's own research programme, students, theoretical expertise, or scientific agenda were fully replaced.

Sources:
- MIT Office Directory: https://officesdirectory.mit.edu/psfc
- MIT News, 21 Apr 2026: https://news.mit.edu/2026/psfc-showcases-technologies-applicable-to-fusion-and-geothermal-0421

### Matched-control seed
A natural organisational control is the preceding PSFC leadership transition. Dennis Whyte stepped down at the end of 2023; Earl Marmar served as interim director; Loureiro became permanent director effective 1 May 2024. This supplies an ordinary leadership-transition baseline against which the sudden 2025 transition can be compared.

Source:
- MIT Office of the Vice President for Research, 22 Mar 2024: https://orgchart.mit.edu/letters/nuno-loureiro-lead-plasma-science-and-fusion-center

### Current empirical reading
- **Programme-level paralysis:** not supported by the located public record.
- **Leadership replacement:** source-backed at interim level.
- **Scientific/tacit replacement:** unresolved.
- **Work-linked motive or actor evidence:** none located in this pass.

## 2. Anthony Chavez

### Event chronology
- New Mexico DPS lists Anthony Chavez as missing from Los Alamos on 8 May 2025.
- A Los Alamos Police Department release states that he was reported missing on 8 May and that exhaustive initial efforts had not located him.

Sources:
- New Mexico DPS missing-person record: https://missingpersons.dps.nm.gov/mpweb/mpdetailreport_serv?id=M99969
- LAPD release reproduced by Los Alamos Reporter, 12 May 2025: https://losalamosreporter.com/2025/05/12/lapd-missing-person/

### Post-loss disruption
- Scorpius did not exhibit obvious programme-level stoppage in the located record.
- LLNL reports that major Scorpius pulsed-power deliveries concluded in September 2025 and January 2026, that production was kept on schedule, and that the project moved toward integrated testing.
- This occurred after Chavez's disappearance.

Source:
- LLNL, 22 Jul 2026, “Milestone shipments power next phases of Scorpius accelerator project”: https://www.llnl.gov/article/54716/milestone-shipments-power-next-phases-scorpius-accelerator-project

### Current empirical reading
- **Whole-programme disruption:** current public evidence cuts against a strong paralysis claim.
- **Chavez-specific tacit loss:** unresolved. The located milestone material does not say whether his particular design responsibilities had already transferred or whether others absorbed them.
- **Active role at disappearance:** unresolved. The LANL profile documents >25 years at DARHT and Scorpius design work but does not by itself establish his exact role on 8 May 2025.

Highest-value next receipt: identify the exact Scorpius subsystem(s) Chavez designed, the ownership/successor after his disappearance, and pre/post-May-2025 schedule baselines.

## 3. Joshua Kyle LeBlanc

### Event chronology
- Funeral records give LeBlanc's death as 22 July 2025.
- Secondary reporting citing the Alabama Law Enforcement Agency describes a single-vehicle Tesla crash followed by fire. The underlying ALEA crash report has not yet been located in this pass.

Sources:
- Dignity Memorial obituary: https://www.dignitymemorial.com/obituaries/jasper-al/joshua-leblanc-12500568
- Fox News report citing ALEA, 22 Apr 2026: https://www.foxnews.com/us/nasa-nuclear-engineer-found-dead-burned-tesla-vanishing-alabama-home-last-year

### Post-loss programme continuity
- NASA NTRS records the 40 kW Fission Surface Power I&C technology-development presentation, with Joshua LeBlanc among the authors, as acquired on 16 August 2025 for a webinar starting 26 August 2025 — after his death.
- NASA held additional Fission Surface Power technology-maturation activity in September 2025.
- NASA and DOE publicly renewed and accelerated the lunar fission-surface-power effort in January 2026.
- NASA TechPort still lists Fission Surface Power as an active technology project in July 2026.

Sources:
- NASA NTRS 20250008475: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250008475
- NASA NTRS 20250008547: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20250008547
- NASA release, 13 Jan 2026: https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-department-of-energy-to-develop-lunar-surface-reactor-by-2030/
- NASA TechPort FSP: https://techport.nasa.gov/projects/105671

### Current empirical reading
- **Whole FSP programme stopped by LeBlanc's death:** contradicted by continued public programme activity.
- **His I&C role was easy to replace:** not established.
- **I&C team redistribution / successor:** unresolved.

Highest-value next receipt: NASA/Marshall organisational records identifying the post-July-2025 SNP/FSP I&C technical lead and whether milestones, responsibilities, or staffing changed.

## 4. William Neil McCasland

### Event chronology
- Public reporting sourced to Bernalillo County Sheriff's Office places McCasland's disappearance on 27 February 2026.
- CBS reported on 12 March 2026 that BCSO had found no evidence of foul play at that point.
- Because McCasland had retired from the USAF in 2013, historical AFRL programme importance cannot be treated as event-time programme disruption.

Sources:
- CBS News, 12 Mar 2026: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/retired-air-force-major-general-missing-new-mexico-william-neil-mccasland/
- Albuquerque Journal, 12 Mar 2026: https://www.abqjournal.com/news/retired-general-was-not-confused-and-disoriented-when-he-went-missing-wife-says/2999479

### Current empirical reading
The next question is not who replaced him as AFRL commander. It is: **what technical, advisory, corporate, consulting, or programme role did he actually occupy in February 2026?** Until that same-object role is identified, a post-loss technical-disruption test is not well-posed.

## 5. Monica Jacinto / Monica Reza

### Event chronology
- LASD-sourced reporting states Monica Reza was last seen on 22 June 2025 near Mount Waterman in Angeles National Forest.
- Search-and-rescue activity involving multiple agencies is documented in contemporaneous reporting.

Source:
- CBS Los Angeles, 23 Jun 2025: https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/angeles-national-forest-missing-hiker-monica-reza/

### Event-time role
- The 20 April 2026 House Oversight letter repeats public reporting that Reza served as director of JPL's Materials Processing Group. That letter is primary evidence for what Congress said, but not an independent JPL personnel record.
- A contemporaneous professional-network post by a JPL colleague likewise described Reza as Director of the Materials Processing Group at NASA JPL, but a primary JPL organisation/personnel record is still preferable.

Sources:
- House Oversight DOE letter, 20 Apr 2026: https://oversight.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/DOE-Missing-Scientists-Letter_4.20.26.pdf
- Allan Petre professional-network post surfaced by search: describes Reza as JPL Materials Processing Group director.

### Current empirical reading
- **Event chronology:** substantially improved.
- **Event-time JPL leadership role:** plausible and multiply reported, but still awaiting a primary institutional personnel receipt.
- **Post-loss programme disruption / successor:** unresolved.
- **Reza-McCasland programme linkage:** remains unclosed; House repeats the allegation rather than independently proving the underlying funding/programme relationship.

Highest-value next receipt: JPL organisation chart/personnel announcement for the Materials Processing Group before and after June 2025, plus the primary AFRL/Air Force Mondaloy award/contract lineage.

## 6. Frank W. Maiwald

### Event chronology and role
- Maiwald's obituary states he died on 4 July 2024 in Los Angeles.
- It says he managed development of the SBG-VSWIR instrument, previously oversaw successful delivery of two AMR-C instruments, and served as a technical group supervisor.
- Colleague memorial testimony describes both deep technical knowledge and management/process expertise, including flight process qualification, assembly/screening techniques, cost/schedule management, and JPL service-centre cost tracking.

Source:
- Frank Werner Maiwald obituary, published 17 Jul 2024: https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/legacyremembers/frank-maiwald-obituary?id=61298603

### Programme continuity
- SBG-VSWIR remained an active JPL/NASA project after his death.
- Current JPL project pages show an active SBG project and current science leadership, but this pass did not locate a source explicitly naming Maiwald's management successor.

Sources:
- JPL SBG project page: https://science.jpl.nasa.gov/projects/sbg-surface-biology-and-geology-designated-observable/
- NASA Science SBG page, updated Mar 2026: https://science.nasa.gov/earth-science/decadal-surveys/decadal-sbg/

### Current empirical reading
- **Instrument/programme continuation:** source-backed.
- **Maiwald-specific management replacement latency:** unresolved.
- **Tacit/process loss:** unusually plausible from colleague testimony, but not yet quantified against controls.

Highest-value next receipt: identify who assumed SBG-VSWIR instrument-development management after 4 July 2024, and compare schedule/budget/reorganisation with the pre-death baseline.

# Cross-case result from this pass

The first direct disruption test does **not** currently show obvious programme collapse in the three cases where public programme continuity can already be inspected:

1. PSFC continued under an interim director after Loureiro.
2. Scorpius continued through major scheduled hardware milestones after Chavez disappeared.
3. NASA Fission Surface Power continued public technology-maturation activity and was accelerated institutionally after LeBlanc's death.

This does **not** refute tacit-knowledge or key-person hypotheses. It narrows them. The stronger claim now has to be person-specific:

- loss of a unique research direction,
- slower replacement than ordinary controls,
- hidden redistribution of technical responsibility,
- loss of a rare qualification or design history,
- or measurable local delay despite programme-level continuation.

Accordingly, the next empirical pass should target **successor identity and local responsibility transfer**, not broad programme survival.
