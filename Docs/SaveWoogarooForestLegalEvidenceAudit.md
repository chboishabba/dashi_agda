# Save Woogaroo Forest — legal evidence source audit

This companion note updates `SaveWoogarooForestLegalTeamBrief.md` with the latest external material. It is a source-diligence note for Save Woogaroo Forest and assisting/pro-bono lawyers, not legal advice.

## 1. What the uploaded `2019-8575-Draft-PD.pdf` actually is

Despite the filename, the supplied Commonwealth PDF is a **one-page invitation for public comment**, not the Preliminary Documentation bundle itself.

It source-pays:

- Springfield Residential Development — EPBC 2019/8575;
- Cherish Enterprises Pty Ltd as the proponent;
- controlled-action status;
- assessment by Preliminary Documentation;
- controlling provisions under EPBC Act ss 18 and 18A;
- the named controlling species/community;
- the 13 February–12 March 2026 public-comment window;
- the fact that hard-copy Preliminary Documentation was made available at Ipswich City Council, State Library of Queensland and DCCEEW;
- the Saunders Havill documentation URL used for the actual documentation.

It **does not** source-pay the project-specific habitat hectares, habitat-quality scores, offsets, corridor-loss calculations or final avoidance/mitigation tables.

Therefore:

```text
2019-8575-Draft-PD.pdf
  !=
Final Preliminary Documentation habitat bundle
```

The final-PD acquisition residual remains open.

## 2. The extension notice is primary and decision-specific

The uploaded National Environmental Protection Agency notice is the strongest current procedural carrier. It says that the period in which the **delegate** must decide whether to approve EPBC 2019/8575 was extended by **20 business days to 1 October 2026** under EPBC Act s 130(1A), and names **Declan O'Connor-Cox, Branch Head, Environment Assessments Queensland** as the person authorised to make the decision.

Any portal UI field such as `Expired`, `Published`, or another status label should not be promoted over that dated primary notice without resolving exactly what the UI field denotes.

```text
portal status label
  !=
dated statutory extension specification
```

## 3. Queensland Statewide Biodiversity Corridors — useful, but not s 13 by itself

The official Queensland spatial service `Statewide Biodiversity Corridors` maps:

- terrestrial corridor centrelines;
- riparian corridor centrelines;
- corridor buffers classified at state/regional level.

The supplied screenshots show this corridor mapping across the broader Brisbane–Ipswich–Springfield landscape and along the Opossum/Woogaroo-connected drainage system.

This is highly relevant to:

- fragmentation;
- landscape connectivity;
- riparian continuity;
- cumulative-impact analysis;
- an NCA s 13 **essentiality** argument when joined to species/population ecology.

But the map is not itself a statutory declaration that the Springview parcel is `critical habitat` under NCA s 13.

```text
Statewide Biodiversity Corridor
  !=
NCA s 13 critical habitat
```

The next spatial task is an **exact project-footprint / cadastral join** against the official corridor layer, not a visual inference from the regional screenshot.

## 4. Commonwealth Register of Critical Habitat — different statutory object

The supplied SPRAT screenshot is the Commonwealth **Register of Critical Habitat**. It is a federal EPBC object. The visible register entries do not include a Woogaroo/koala entry.

This does not weaken a Queensland s 13 argument because the two objects are different legal types:

```text
EPBC Register of Critical Habitat
  !=
Queensland NCA s 13 critical habitat
```

A missing Commonwealth register entry must not be treated as proof that habitat is not critical for another statutory consumer.

## 5. Declared Fish Habitat Areas — separate fisheries regime

The supplied Business Queensland page concerns **declared Fish Habitat Areas** under Queensland fisheries legislation. These are separately declared and mapped coastal/fisheries-protection objects.

They may be useful where a project actually intersects a declared FHA, but they are not interchangeable with terrestrial wildlife critical habitat under the Nature Conservation Act.

```text
Fish Habitat Area
  !=
terrestrial NCA critical habitat
```

No Woogaroo terrestrial preservation argument should consume the FHA regime unless a separate spatial/statutory bridge is proven.

It is therefore not Queensland evidence and should be excluded from the Woogaroo parcel matrix.

```text
South Australian biodiversity dataset
  !=

```

## 8. Project-outline map compared with the Queensland corridor overlay

A campaign-supplied aerial outline map identifies, from north to south, **Bellevue Woods**, **Springview Village 2 & 3**, **Scenic Precinct** and **Peninsular Precinct**, with Brookwater to the west and Camira / the military training area to the east. The Springview, Scenic and Peninsular polygons appear as a near-continuous wooded north–south chain rather than isolated patches.

The previously supplied official Queensland Statewide Biodiversity Corridors screenshots independently show a north–south riparian corridor through the Goodna–Redbank Plains–Brookwater–Springfield landscape, together with state/regional corridor buffers in the broader Springfield area.

A visual comparison therefore creates a **strong spatial acquisition lead**:

```text
campaign project-outline chain
  +
official Queensland corridor surface
  ->
candidate exact project x corridor intersection
```

The apparent relationship is strongest for the Springview / Scenic part of the chain and remains relevant to Peninsular at the southern transition into the broader Springfield corridor surface.

However, these are screenshots at different scales and are not a georeferenced overlay. Therefore:

```text
visual alignment
  !=
exact GIS intersection
```

and:

```text
forest continuity visible in aerial imagery
  !=
statutory critical-habitat finding
```

The legal/ecological value comes from performing the exact join and then asking what function the intersected habitat performs for the relevant population/community.

This could produce the useful same-object chain:

```text

```

for the EPBC and NCA consumers.

### Public-education context is supportive, not legal evidence

The campaign's `Maximus's Forest — Why Habitats Matter` teaching material already introduces older students to **habitat fragmentation** and **wildlife corridors**, and frames the planning problem as whether housing/roads can coexist with habitat, green space, corridors and buffers. That makes corridor/connectivity a coherent existing campaign narrative rather than a newly invented litigation framing.

But:

```text
campaign teaching material
  !=
ecological expert evidence
  !=
official GIS fact
  !=
legal conclusion
```

It belongs in communications/community context, while the official corridor dataset, exact project geometry and ecological evidence belong in the legal proof package.

The useful question for counsel/ecologists is no longer merely whether the land is mapped as a corridor. It is:

> **If the exact Springview / Scenic / Peninsular polygons intersect the official Queensland riparian or state/regional biodiversity-corridor geometry, what legal and ecological weight should that same-object intersection carry in EPBC fragmentation/cumulative-impact analysis and in an NCA s 13 case that the affected habitat is essential to conservation of a viable local population/community?**

That question keeps the aerial outline and corridor screenshot in the correct role: they identify a high-value spatial hypothesis. The hypothesis becomes evidence only when the exact geometries are joined and source-receipted.
