# Save Woogaroo Forest — legal evidence source audit

This companion note updates `SaveWoogarooForestLegalTeamBrief.md` with the latest external material. It is a source-diligence note for Save Woogaroo Forest and assisting/pro-bono lawyers, not legal advice.

## 1. What the uploaded `2019-8575-Draft-PD.pdf` actually is

Despite the filename, the supplied Commonwealth PDF is a **one-page invitation for public comment**, not the Preliminary Documentation bundle itself.

It source-pays:

- Springfield Residential Development — EPBC 2019/8575;
- Cherish Enterprises Pty Ltd as the proponent;
- controlled-action status;
- assessment by Preliminary Documentation;
- controlling provisions under EPBC Act ss 18 and 18A;
- the named controlling species/community;
- the 13 February–12 March 2026 public-comment window;
- the fact that hard-copy Preliminary Documentation was made available at Ipswich City Council, State Library of Queensland and DCCEEW;
- the Saunders Havill documentation URL used for the actual documentation.

It **does not** source-pay the project-specific habitat hectares, habitat-quality scores, offsets, corridor-loss calculations or final avoidance/mitigation tables.

Therefore:

```text
2019-8575-Draft-PD.pdf
  !=
Final Preliminary Documentation habitat bundle
```

The final-PD acquisition residual remains open.

## 3. Queensland Statewide Biodiversity Corridors — useful, but not s 13 by itself

The official Queensland spatial service `Statewide Biodiversity Corridors` maps:

- terrestrial corridor centrelines;
- riparian corridor centrelines;
- corridor buffers classified at state/regional level.

The supplied screenshots show this corridor mapping across the broader Brisbane–Ipswich–Springfield landscape and along the Opossum/Woogaroo-connected drainage system.

This is highly relevant to:

- fragmentation;
- landscape connectivity;
- riparian continuity;
- cumulative-impact analysis;
- an NCA s 13 **essentiality** argument when joined to species/population ecology.

But the map is not itself a statutory declaration that the Springview parcel is `critical habitat` under NCA s 13.

```text
Statewide Biodiversity Corridor
  !=
NCA s 13 critical habitat
```

The next spatial task is an **exact project-footprint / cadastral join** against the official corridor layer, not a visual inference from the regional screenshot.

## 4. Commonwealth Register of Critical Habitat — different statutory object

The supplied SPRAT screenshot is the Commonwealth **Register of Critical Habitat**. It is a federal EPBC object. The visible register entries do not include a Woogaroo/koala entry.

This does not weaken a Queensland s 13 argument because the two objects are different legal types:

```text
EPBC Register of Critical Habitat
  !=
Queensland NCA s 13 critical habitat
```

A missing Commonwealth register entry must not be treated as proof that habitat is not critical for another statutory consumer.

## 5. Declared Fish Habitat Areas — separate fisheries regime

The supplied Business Queensland page concerns **declared Fish Habitat Areas** under Queensland fisheries legislation. These are separately declared and mapped coastal/fisheries-protection objects.

They may be useful where a project actually intersects a declared FHA, but they are not interchangeable with terrestrial wildlife critical habitat under the Nature Conservation Act.

```text
Fish Habitat Area
  !=
terrestrial NCA critical habitat
```

No Woogaroo terrestrial preservation argument should consume the FHA regime unless a separate spatial/statutory bridge is proven.

It is therefore not Queensland evidence and should be excluded from the Woogaroo parcel matrix.

```text
South Australian biodiversity dataset
  !=

```

## 9. Lawyer-facing question created by the new corridor evidence

The useful question for counsel/ecologists is no longer merely whether the land is mapped as a corridor. It is:

> **What legal and ecological weight should be given to an official Queensland state/regional biodiversity-corridor intersection with the exact Springview footprint when assessing EPBC fragmentation/cumulative impact and when building an NCA s 13 case that the habitat is essential to conservation of a viable local population/community?**

That question keeps the corridor map in the correct role: strong, official **connectivity evidence**, but not a self-executing statutory conclusion.
