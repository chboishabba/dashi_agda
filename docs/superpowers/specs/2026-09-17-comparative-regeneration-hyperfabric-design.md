# Comparative Regeneration Hyperfabric Design

Date: 2026-09-17
Status: approved in-chat architecture; implementation pending written-spec review

## Goal

Extend the existing Acacia/Senegalia BNF–LES work into a comparative regeneration fabric that treats Australian native legumes/wattles, native grassland succession, mine/old-field rehabilitation, and sorghum–sudangrass nurse/cover systems as distinct empirical systems that can share typed functional roles without being collapsed into one ecology.

The target composition is:

```text
Australian native-legume / wattle rhizobial restoration
        -> native-soil-biota / mine rehabilitation
        -> Australian old-field / grassland succession
        -> temporary sorghum–sudangrass nurse / cover functions
        -> existing Acacia/Senegalia BNF + dryland LES machinery
        -> comparative regeneration hyperfabric
```

The hyperfabric must preserve:

```text
source proposition
!= source identity metadata
!= DASHI typed reconstruction
!= DASHI synthetic collision
!= cross-source functional analogy
!= deployment authority
```

## Source acquisition set

### Australian native legumes / wattles / rhizobia

1. Burdon, Gibson, Searle, Woods & Brockwell (1999), `Variation in the effectiveness of symbiotic associations between native rhizobia and temperate Australian Acacia: within-species interactions`, Journal of Applied Ecology 36:398-408. DOI `10.1046/j.1365-2664.1999.00409.x`.
   - Role: within-species host-population × rhizobial-isolate effectiveness.
   - Bounded reading: strong variation among Acacia–rhizobial combinations; average performance does not define a universal effective strain.
   - Exclusion: glasshouse host-growth effectiveness is not field establishment, survival, whole-community recovery or deployment authority.

2. Thrall, Burdon & Woods (2000), `Variation in the effectiveness of symbiotic associations between native rhizobia and temperate Australian legumes: interactions within and between genera`, Journal of Applied Ecology 37:52-65. DOI `10.1046/j.1365-2664.2000.00470.x`.
   - Role: across-Acacia and across-native-legume host-specificity donor, including Acacia, Hardenbergia, Daviesia and Indigofera.
   - Bounded reading: rhizobial performance can transfer unevenly among hosts/genera; rare/restricted hosts may require greater strain attention.
   - Exclusion: taxonomic proximity is not an exact strain-selection theorem and cross-host effectiveness does not imply same community role.

3. Murray, Thrall & Woods (2001), `Acacia species and rhizobial interactions: Implications for restoration of native vegetation`, Ecological Management & Restoration 2:213-219. DOI `10.1046/j.1442-8903.2001.00086.x`.
   - Role: restoration interpretation of Australian Acacia–rhizobia interaction evidence.
   - Exclusion: interpretive/review framing must not override primary experiment ownership.

4. Thrall et al. (2005), `Seed inoculation with effective root-nodule bacteria enhances revegetation success`, Journal of Applied Ecology 42:740-751. DOI `10.1111/j.1365-2664.2005.01058.x`.
   - Role: large-scale south-eastern Australian direct-seeding field restoration.
   - Bounded reading: inoculation increased establishment on average, often increased early survival/growth, and effects varied among species/sites; no fertilizer or supplementary watering was applied in the reported direct-seeding trials.
   - Exclusion: establishment, growth and survival are distinct outcomes; early revegetation success is not self-regenerating community recovery or universal inoculant effectiveness.

### Australian soil-biota / mine rehabilitation

5. Bell, Wells, Jasper & Abbott (2003), `Field inoculation with arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi in rehabilitation of mine sites with native vegetation, including Acacia spp.`, Australian Systematic Botany 16:131-138. DOI `10.1071/SB02004`.
   - Role: Western Australian mine-site AM-fungal inoculation and indigenous-propagule context.
   - Bounded reading: viable introduced AM inoculum did not guarantee extensive native-plant colonisation or growth benefit where indigenous propagules/environmental constraints remained important.
   - Exclusion: inoculum viability != colonisation != growth benefit != rehabilitation success.

Additional Australian mine/revegetation microbiome sources may be acquired only when they add a named coordinate such as cognate-vs-agricultural inoculum, local soil-biota legacy, mine-substrate filtering, or field survival.

### Australian grassland / old-field succession

6. Scott & Morgan (2012), `Recovery of soil and vegetation in semi-arid Australian old fields`, Journal of Arid Environments 76:61-71. DOI `10.1016/j.jaridenv.2011.08.014`.
   - Role: ~100-year semi-arid old-field chronosequence.
   - Bounded reading: soil and vegetation recovery can proceed at different rates; native perennial grasses can replace early exotic dominance over long time spans.
   - Exclusion: chronosequence convergence is not exact temporal replay of one field and soil recovery is not compositional identity.

7. Fensham, Butler, Fairfax, Quintin & Dwyer (2016), `Passive restoration of subtropical grassland after abandonment of cultivation`, Journal of Applied Ecology 53:274-283. DOI `10.1111/1365-2664.12551`.
   - Role: Queensland subtropical-grassland passive restoration / dispersal constraint.
   - Bounded reading: passive recovery can work where remnant seed sources exist and deflected succession/exotic monopolisation are limited; dispersal traits strongly affect recovery rates.
   - Exclusion: abandonment alone does not determine recovery and nearby remnant presence does not guarantee all species return.

8. Parkhurst, Standish & Prober (2022), `P is for persistence: Soil phosphorus remains elevated for more than a decade after old field restoration`, Ecological Applications 32:e2547. DOI `10.1002/eap.2547`; PMID `35080806`.
   - Role: persistent agricultural nutrient legacy in semi-arid Western Australian restoration.
   - Bounded reading: elevated soil P can persist after restoration planting and remain a filter on native recolonisation.
   - Exclusion: current vegetation or restoration age does not identify recovered soil nutrient state.

Additional old-field/grassland sources may be acquired when they explicitly pay seed limitation, exotic competition, grazing/fire management, dispersal, soil-biota recovery, or trajectory-divergence coordinates.

### Sorghum–sudangrass / temporary nurse and cover functions

9. Kaneko et al. (2023), `Hybrid brachiariagrass establishment with annual sorghum sudangrass and sunn hemp mixtures at half and full recommended seeding rates`, Grassland Science. DOI `10.1111/grs.12391`.
   - Role: temporary annual-forage companion during slow perennial-pasture establishment.
   - Bounded reading: annual companion vegetation can alter first-year ground cover/forage and establishment trade-offs.
   - Exclusion: temporary annual biomass or companion establishment is not native-grassland restoration and not a universal nurse effect.

10. Guretzky & Redfearn (2021), `Seeding Rate Effects on Forage Mass and Vegetation Dynamics of Cool-Season Grass Sod Interseeded with Sorghum-Sudangrass`, Agronomy 11:2449. DOI `10.3390/agronomy11122449`.
   - Role: annual warm-season interseeding into established perennial sod.
   - Bounded reading: sorghum–sudangrass can increase seasonal forage mass without necessarily leaving residual vegetation effects after the annual phase.
   - Exclusion: seasonal biomass gain != persistent community transition.

11. Burt, Soder & Mercier (2025), `Interseeding crabgrass and berseem clover into sorghum–sudangrass for improved herbage accumulation, nutritive value, and weed suppression`, Crop, Forage & Turfgrass Management 11:e70055. DOI `10.1002/cft2.70055`.
   - Role: annual-mixture weed-suppression / biomass-function donor.
   - Exclusion: weed suppression, forage quality and herbage accumulation are separate from biodiversity restoration or perennial-system recovery.

12. `Summer cover crop mixes: Part 2—Weed suppression and early cash crop performance`, Agronomy Journal (2025/2026). DOI `10.1002/agj2.70236`.
   - Role: high-biomass summer-grass mixtures including sorghum–sudangrass as weed-suppression donors.
   - Exclusion: weed suppression or cash-crop facilitation is not ecological restoration authority.

Extension/fact-sheet material may support vocabulary such as nutrient scavenging, compaction relief or nurse-crop practice, but theorem-bearing claims should prefer peer-reviewed primary studies.

## Architecture

### 1. `AustralianNativeLegumeRhizobiaRestorationExact`

Source-bearing owner for Australian native legume/wattle rhizobial effectiveness and field restoration.

Required coordinates:

```text
host species
host population / provenance
rhizobial isolate / strain
isolate origin
soil / site
experimental stage (glasshouse vs field)
inoculation treatment
establishment
growth
survival
source / observer role
```

Required no-promotion laws:

```text
strain identity != symbiotic effectiveness
symbiotic effectiveness != field establishment
field establishment != survival
early growth != community recovery
local/native strain label != universal best strain
```

Required finite information-loss witness:

```text
RestorationPerformance
  not FactorsThrough
RhizobialStrainIdentity
```

with worlds differing in host population/species/site while retaining the same projected strain token.

### 2. `AustralianWattleSoilBiotaRehabilitationExact`

Source-bearing owner for AM-fungal / broader soil-biota rehabilitation evidence.

Required distinctions:

```text
introduced inoculum
indigenous propagule pool
inoculum viability
root colonisation
plant P status
early plant growth
field rehabilitation outcome
```

Required firewalls:

```text
viable inoculum != realised colonisation
realised colonisation != growth benefit
introduced inoculum != missing indigenous propagule pool
successful nursery/assay infection != field rehabilitation
```

This owner may reuse the existing `AcaciaSenegalDualSymbiosisPContextExact` role distinctions but must not fuse Australian mine-site measurements with Senegal restoration measurements.

### 3. `AustralianGrasslandSuccessionRegenerationExact`

Owner for passive/active grassland and old-field trajectory evidence.

Required coordinates:

```text
current vegetation state
restoration / abandonment age
seed-source proximity
dispersal trait / seed limitation
exotic competition
soil nutrient legacy
soil recovery state
native richness
floristic composition
perennial-grass recovery
management / disturbance history
```

Required collisions:

```text
SoilRecovery not equivalent to FloristicRecovery
PresentVegetation not FactorsThrough RestorationAge
CommunityRecovery not FactorsThrough SoilRecovery
PassiveRestorationSuccess not FactorsThrough AbandonmentAlone
```

Historical agricultural P must remain a latent state coordinate even after native planting.

### 4. `SudangrassNurseCoverCropExact`

Treat sorghum–sudangrass as an intervention/function source, not as a native-restoration species.

Required role coordinates:

```text
temporary ground cover
annual forage biomass
weed suppression
nutrient scavenging where source-paid
companion/nurse function
perennial establishment response
termination / persistence
subsequent vegetation response
```

Required firewalls:

```text
temporary cover != perennial recovery
biomass != biodiversity
weed suppression != restoration
forage gain != trajectory success
nurse role != ecological equivalence with native pioneer species
```

The owner must encode reversibility/termination explicitly: a nurse crop can be useful because it is temporary, and persistence beyond the intended window can become a different intervention state.

### 5. `DrylandPioneerLegumeGrasslandRegenerationHyperfabricExact`

Cross-system DASHI synthesis owner.

It does **not** compare taxa directly. It compares typed functional roles and the information needed for consumer questions.

Minimum functional-role vocabulary:

```text
nitrogenFixingPioneer
temporaryNurse
groundCoverProvider
weedCompetitor
soilBiotaCarrier
deepRootedHydrologicalActor

```

Minimum empirical systems:

```text
Australian native legume / wattle revegetation
Australian mine-site rehabilitation
Australian old-field / native-grassland succession
Senegalia senegal dryland agroforestry / BNF LES
sorghum–sudangrass annual nurse / cover systems
```

Required global firewalls:

```text
same functional role != same ecological object
same role label != transferable effect size
pioneer establishment != desired endpoint
short-term intervention success != long-term trajectory success
soil improvement != community restoration
N-fixing species presence != realised N contribution
high biomass != high restoration value
```

Primary temporal theorem target:

```text
TrajectorySuccessAtT2
  not FactorsThrough
InterventionSuccessAtT1
```

Primary role-transfer theorem target:

```text
RestorationOutcome
  not FactorsThrough
FunctionalRoleLabel
```

Both are DASHI synthetic non-factorability witnesses. External sources own only the bounded empirical propositions that motivate the differing worlds.

## Attribution rules

For every source-bearing Agda owner:

1. attach DOI through `DASHI.Core.AttributedSourceCore` when verified;
2. attach PMID/PMCID only when independently verified;
3. retain source geography, host/taxon and experimental role;
4. mark review/interpretive papers as such and do not let them override primary-source ownership;
5. distinguish source proposition from DASHI finite witness;
6. do not use shared species names, functional-role labels, geography or restoration vocabulary as implicit same-object joins;
7. Australian Acacia/wattle evidence remains Australian source evidence; Senegal/Sudan Senegalia evidence remains source-local; sudangrass agronomy remains agronomic evidence unless a restoration study directly pays a restoration proposition.

## Success criteria

The tranche is successful when:

1. Australian native legumes/wattles are first-class source owners rather than analogy footnotes;
2. inoculation is separated from effectiveness, establishment, survival and long-term regeneration;
3. mine/soil-biota rehabilitation retains indigenous-propagule and colonisation context;
4. grassland succession retains seed limitation, exotic competition and persistent agricultural soil legacies;
5. sorghum–sudangrass is typed as a temporary/reversible nurse-cover intervention, not native restoration vegetation;
6. the comparative hyperfabric proves at least one temporal and one role-transfer non-factorability theorem;
7. no source scope, geography, taxon or functional-role analogy is silently promoted across systems;
8. the existing BNF ladder remains authoritative and unchanged.
