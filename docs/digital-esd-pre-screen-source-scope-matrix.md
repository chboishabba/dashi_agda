## Interpretation rule

The matrix separates **what a source can legitimately contribute** from what remains unpaid for a downstream consumer:

- acquisition is not inclusion;
- institutional authority is not intervention-effect evidence;
- adopted statements are not interchangeable with consultation/background papers;
- procurement/TCO background papers define candidate coordinates without supplying deployment measurements;
- small empirical/case studies retain their study population and design limits rather than becoming universal rules;
- Chugh already pays the reverse-sustainability paradox and Böhme already pays the coupled twin-transformation premise, so neither proposition should be claimed as novel in bare form;
- close conceptual antecedents narrow the contribution claim but neither establish duplication nor publication novelty by themselves;
- the UNECE regional evaluation pays an implementation-gap premise, not a digital-ESD intervention effect;
- longitudinal input monitoring is not equivalent to process, output or outcome transformation;
- method standards specify coordinates but do not supply measurements;
- normative interoperability guidance does not prove deployed persistence;
- participant/public-governance evidence does not create local constitutive authority; and
- activity, adoption and task performance remain distinct from system transformation and learning.

The matrix may survive into structured extraction as provenance/context metadata **only if** the corresponding source is admitted through the completed search and screening lineage.
