## 1. Consumer-relative material allocation

The existing material-substrate audit identifies lifecycle stages and affected carriers, but a shared digital/AI burden still needs an explicit allocation rule before it can be stated per request, learner, task or course.

`DigitalESDMaterialImpactAllocationExact` therefore asks:

1. what is the functional unit?
2. what shared burden is being allocated?
3. what allocation basis is used?
4. what lifetime/usage denominator is assumed or observed?
5. what resource-time/utilisation share belongs to the unit?
6. how are training, serving and embodied burdens separated?
7. whose definition of a task/consumer determines the denominator?
8. which lifecycle burdens remain outside the allocated unit?

The owner reuses the existing AI training/serving economics separation rather than adding another generic amortisation calculus. It also reuses the material-substrate, externality-incidence and dialectical-material-revision boundaries.

Primary calibration sources are EcoLogits (Rincé & Banse, 2025, DOI 10.21105/joss.07471) and Woo (2025, DOI 10.1038/s41598-025-24658-5). Woo's request-level AI LCA uses one inference request as the functional unit and allocates embodied server/GPU impact proportionally to request resource time over expected hardware lifetime; training and end-of-life remain outside that request-level system boundary.

Constructive DASHI collisions prove:

```text
per-consumer impact not FactorsThrough total shared impact
per-consumer impact not FactorsThrough consumer count
```

Hence:

```text
more consumers != automatically lower absolute burden
lower allocated per-request burden != lower aggregate burden
amortisation rule != known total future usage
allocation accounting != burden-incidence justice
```

The Marx/Hegel/dialectical-material lane is used only as a generic structural donor: changed material conditions may revise the relevant relation/position, while a material accounting result does not create a historical Marxist interpretation. 369 is not imported because no current consumer collision requires it.
