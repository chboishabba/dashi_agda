# Digital-ESD candidate synthesis working draft

**Status:** pre-screen analytical working draft. This document synthesises source-bounded candidate contributions already acquired in the repository. It is **not** the final review Results section, does not claim evidence completeness, and must be challenged and re-run against the eventual searched, screened and extracted corpus.

## 1. From a mature digital-education corpus to candidate ESD principles

The paper's generative centre is not a claim that digital technology automatically advances sustainability. It is the more constrained question of what established digital-education research can legitimately contribute to transformative ESD.

The current Alice Brown / colleague corpus contains distinct source fibres on feedback classification, online engagement and humour, student voice and epistemic agency, student-identified support, ecology of data, parent allyship and partnership, and situated negotiation/independent mobility. Read separately, these sources do not state a unified sustainability framework. Read together with their provenance preserved, however, they support a **candidate cross-paper synthesis** around seven transferable principles:

1. **Situated relational engagement** — design for meaningful interaction in context rather than access or technology presence alone.
2. **Feedback as revisable signal** — treat feedback analytics and machine classifications as contestable signals, not student meaning or pedagogical authority.
3. **Constitutive learner agency** — enable learners to shape questions, categories, interpretation, design and evidence return.
4. **Adaptive support with local choice** — route support through situated needs and learner choice rather than fixed intervention tables.
5. **Plural situated observers** — retain learner, teacher, family/ally, researcher, institution and model perspectives without treating one as the complete system view.
6. **Contextual custodianship** — retain person-place relations, affordances, effort and flows of value when interpreting educational data.
7. **Iterative evidence return and recharting** — return evidence for contestation and revise classifications, support choices and institutional decisions as new information appears.

These principles are **DASHI cross-paper synthesis**, not empirical findings attributed to the individual source papers. They also do not yet create sustainability evidence.

The corpus is especially useful because it repeatedly follows a developmental movement:

\[
\text{proxy or engagement signal}
\rightarrow
\text{restore context}
\rightarrow
\text{redistribute agency}
\rightarrow
\text{route local support}
\rightarrow
\text{retain plural observers}
\rightarrow
\text{return evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{revise the chart}.
\]

This pattern is closer to educational transformation than a list of technologies because it describes how interpretation, pedagogy and institutional response can change.

## 2. Enactment sits between principle and sustainability

The seven principles are not self-executing. The canonical transformation layer retains four distinct enactment/scaling conditions:

\[
\boxed{
\text{scalable pedagogy}
+
\text{institutional practice}
+
\text{professional development}
+
\text{policy}.
}
\]

Professional development is therefore not treated as an eighth principle. It is a condition on whether several principles—especially situated engagement, feedback interpretation, adaptive support, plural-observer practice, contextual custodianship and iterative revision—can be enacted consistently beyond isolated examples.

The architecture is now:

\[
\boxed{
\text{source-attributed pedagogical principle}
\rightarrow
\text{enactment/scaling conditions}
\rightarrow
\text{independent sustainability constraints}.
}
\]

This separation is important. Naming professional development does not prove implementation capacity, just as naming a pedagogical mechanism does not prove sustainability.

## 3. Digital activity, integration and transformation remain different levels

The candidate evidence supports a distinction between the presence of digital technology and the educational mechanisms through which sustainability capabilities may develop. OECD's *Digital Education Outlook 2026* separates improved task performance with general-purpose generative AI from durable learning, emphasising pedagogical purpose, human-centred teaching, research, governance and enabling infrastructure as conditions on educational benefit.

A more direct digital-ESD mechanism appears in Ardila Echeverry, Gauthier, Hartikainen and Vasalou (2025; DOI 10.3390/su17104289). Their reflective case study followed two five-member postgraduate higher-education teams during a ten-week design-thinking module in which students co-designed digital educational technologies around sustainability challenges. Particular practices—including user-specific literature review, research with target users, and reflection on sustainability values and design decisions—could foster or hinder sustainability competencies. This remains a small, specific postgraduate context rather than a universal learning effect.

Martínez García and colleagues (2026; DOI 10.3390/su18157979) add a wider systematic-review comparator from 33 empirical studies in face-to-face compulsory schooling. Their review shows substantial ambiguity between digital education, ICT use and digital competence and indicates that educational benefits depend on pedagogical intentionality, teacher mediation, policy/infrastructure and equitable access. Its Scopus and Web of Science execution belongs to that review and does not pay the present manuscript's database receipts.

Böhme (2026; DOI 10.3390/educsci16050721) explicitly reconstructs sustainability/ESD and digitality as a mutually coupled **twin transformation** in education. This is close prior conceptual work: the present manuscript should not claim novelty for the bare idea that sustainability and digital transformation should be integrated reciprocally.

At the implementation level, the UNECE fifth evaluation report synthesises 31 national reports from the 2021–2025 implementation phase. It reports growing use of digital platforms, tools, access initiatives and digital resources while also identifying limited and uneven intentional integration of sustainability principles into digital-education policy and practice. Thus digitalisation and ESD can both progress without becoming an integrated digital-ESD transformation.

UNESCO's 2026 mid-term evaluation independently reports substantial programme implementation activity alongside limited systemic transformation. Holst, Singer-Brodowski, Brock and de Haan (2024; DOI 10.1002/sd.2865) add a sharper monitoring architecture: across more than 11,000 documents and ten years, they operationalise the **depth** and **speed** of ESD integration while distinguishing input monitoring from process, output and outcome.

Zagami (2026; DOI 10.1080/17439884.2026.2683480) supplies another level through four Australian edtech trajectories shaped by governance, funding, legitimacy and sustainable capital rather than technical novelty alone. This adds institutional-durability context without establishing a universal law of edtech success.

Taken together:

\[
\text{technology presence}
\neq
\text{implementation activity}
\neq
\text{input-level integration}
\neq
\text{task performance}
\neq
\text{learning/outcomes}
\neq
\text{institutional durability}
\neq
\text{system transformation}.
\]

## 4. Sustainability cross-pollination constrains rather than rewrites Corpus A

Chugh (2026; DOI 10.1057/s41599-026-06845-5) is a close antecedent for the **sustainability paradox**: educational digitalisation can expand access and participation while also producing environmental and social costs, motivating lifecycle, procurement and circularity responses. The manuscript therefore should not claim novelty for the bare proposition that digital education itself must be sustainable.

The sustainability corpus adds independent constraints to the seven candidate principles across at least seven dimensions:

- social equity and inclusion;
- epistemic agency and public governance;
- environmental lifecycle and circularity;
- economic total cost of ownership and procurement;
- infrastructure openness, interoperability, portability and exit;
- institutional durability and monitoring; and
- intergenerational option preservation.

The UNESCO-UNICEF-ITU *Charter for Public Digital Learning Platforms* identifies public accountability, inclusion, pedagogical purpose, complementarity, openness, interoperability and trustworthiness as platform-governance principles. The September 2026 UNESCO ministerial statement adds an adopted intergovernmental layer concerning deliberative governance, rights, public accountability and responsible AI/digital infrastructure decisions. UNESCO consultation background papers separately frame procurement as a governance lever and total cost of ownership as broader than purchase price. These background papers remain consultation inputs, not adopted policy.

The environmental measurement boundary is stronger still. ITU-T L.1410 supplies methodology for life-cycle assessment of ICT goods, networks and services; ITU-T L.1023 supplies circularity coordinates including durability, repair, reuse, recycling and upgradeability. These standards identify what needs to be measured but do not manufacture a same-object inventory or sustainability score for a digital-education deployment.

This cross-pollination produces design propositions, not empirical effects. For example:

- **situated relational engagement** is constrained by equity, inclusion, professional capability and institutional durability;
- **feedback as revisable signal** is constrained by epistemic governance, human review, rights and monitoring;
- **constitutive learner agency** is constrained by public accountability and cannot be inferred from mere consultation;
- **adaptive support with local choice** must also confront resource durability and TCO;
- **plural situated observers** makes burden distribution visible, including surveillance, exclusion and material externalities;
- **contextual custodianship** expands educational ecology to devices, networks, energy, repair, procurement, portability and vendor dependence; and
- **iterative evidence return** becomes a long-horizon institutional obligation to monitor, revise and preserve migration/exit options.

The central provenance rule is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Alice/colleague pedagogical support}
\neq
\text{sustainability constraint evidence}.
}
\]

They meet only in the candidate synthesis cell.

## 5. Platform governance, participant experience and epistemic agency

Gouseti and Shaw (2026; DOI 10.1080/17439884.2026.2653746) report qualitative findings from leaders, teachers, students and parents in two English secondary schools. Participants valued streamlined administration, teaching/learning support and communication, while the study also identified monitoring and surveillance, digital exclusion and impacts on teacher digital wellbeing. The study remains a two-school qualitative context, not a universal platformisation result.

The Alice/colleague corpus supplies a stronger governance distinction than user experience alone. Student feedback, surveys and data provision can be useful without constituting epistemic agency. The voice/agency work instead motivates a ladder from feedback source toward question contribution, coding-frame contestation, co-interpretation, co-design and evidence-return review.

The parental allyship/partnership corpus adds plural observer surfaces: student, parent ally, teacher, researcher, institution and model. No one surface is the whole system, and proximity to experience matters. Parent expertise may be relevant without replacing the learner's primary experience; institutional records may be relevant without proving enactment; model outputs may be relevant without becoming human meaning.

The practical design questions become:

> **Who participates? Who interprets? Who benefits? Who bears the externalities? Who can contest the categories? Who has meaningful choice or exit?**

## 6. Contextual custodianship is where pedagogy and material sustainability meet

The ecology-of-data fibre is unusually productive for digital ESD because it already asks analysts to retain system edges, affordances of place, minutiae and effort, value flows and pedagogical curation. In the educational source corpus this concerns interpretation of situated data. Under sustainability cross-pollination, the same custodial orientation expands to material and institutional infrastructure.

Thus devices, networks, energy use, service life, repair, procurement, interoperability, portability, vendor dependence, labour and attention become part of the educational ecology rather than external engineering details.

This does **not** mean the ecology-of-data paper made claims about ICT lifecycle assessment. It means its source-bounded concern with context and value flows can be paired with independently sourced lifecycle/governance evidence to formulate a new candidate principle.

Education-specific contextual evidence in the repository strengthens this boundary. Pinzone, Sarti and Amodeo (2026; DOI 10.1007/s11367-026-02656-7) conduct a life-cycle assessment of higher-education delivery scenarios, while Radovan, Košmerl and Makovec Radovan (2026; DOI 10.3390/su18147213) systematically review environmental impacts of generative AI in education. Neither source justifies a universal ranking of online, hybrid or face-to-face education.

## 7. Durability and longitudinal evidence remain the weakest same-object lane

The current candidate set contains useful monitoring and institutional precedents but comparatively little evidence that follows a **digital-ESD intervention itself** through sustained institutionalisation, infrastructure support windows, replacement cycles and long-horizon educational outcomes.

Holst et al. provide ten-year monitoring of ESD integration at the system-input level. Zagami provides historical trajectories of edtech institutions and platforms. Existing ESD sources include a one-year longitudinal intervention benchmark, while UNESCO's mid-term evaluation provides programme-level evidence across 2021–2024. These improve the methodological basis for RQ5 but observe different objects.

The final paper should distinguish:

\[
\text{longitudinal input integration}
\neq
\text{follow-up outcome}
\neq
\text{institutionalisation}
\neq
\text{platform/service persistence}
\neq
\text{hardware/service-life durability}
\neq
\text{intergenerational option preservation}.
\]

The missing same-object digital-ESD longitudinal carrier remains an explicit residual.

## 8. Principle derivation precedes, but does not replace, structured search

The manuscript currently uses a six-stage derivation method:

1. source-bound corpus extraction;
2. cross-paper relation synthesis;
3. candidate principle derivation;
4. scaling-condition attachment;
5. sustainability-constraint pairing; and
6. structured-corpus challenge and revision.

This means principle generation may legitimately precede database-search closure, because the paper is transparent that the first five stages form a **provisional conceptual framework** rather than final review results.

The sixth stage remains unpaid. After database execution, deduplication, screening and structured extraction, the broader corpus must be allowed to support, narrow, split, merge, defeat or extend the seven candidate principles. The review is therefore designed to challenge the expert-corpus synthesis rather than merely search for confirming examples.

Formally and conceptually:

\[
\boxed{
\text{pre-search candidate framework}
\neq
\text{final evidence synthesis}.
}
\]

## 9. Contribution positioning after the close-antecedent snowball

The novelty snowball materially contracts the defensible claim.

**Chugh (2026)** already pays the reverse sustainability-paradox premise.

**Böhme (2026)** already pays a coupled twin-transformation premise.

The paper therefore should claim novelty for neither proposition in bare form.

The remaining candidate contribution is the **source-attributed transfer architecture**:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{mature digital-education corpus}\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{source-attributed synthesis}}
\text{candidate pedagogical principles}\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{enactment conditions}}
\text{institutionally actionable candidates}\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{independent sustainability constraints}}
\text{digital-ESD framework}\\
&\xrightarrow{\text{searched-corpus challenge}}
\text{revised review synthesis}.
\end{aligned}}
}
\]

Its distinctive discipline lies in retaining:

- technology/activity/integration/performance/learning/durability/transformation separations;
- source role, population/context and time horizon;
- same-object versus contextual evidence status;
- participant-authority and context-transfer boundaries;
- professional-development and institutional enactment conditions;
- lifecycle/circularity method-versus-measurement residuals; and
- non-skippable search → corpus → extraction → synthesis lineage.

This is still a **candidate contribution position**, not a global novelty claim. Publication novelty remains open until the declared search and screening are complete.

## 10. Working synthesis claim

The strongest current formulation is conditional:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Transformative digital ESD}
=
\text{credible pedagogical mechanism}
\times
\text{enactment capacity}
\times
\text{sustainability/governance admissibility}
\times
\text{scope-appropriate evidence}.
}
\]

The source corpus gives us something more interesting than a catalogue of technologies: a provisional theory of **how educational transformation should remain relational, revisable, participatory, context-sensitive and institutionally learnable**. The sustainability corpus then asks whether those educational mechanisms also survive material, social, economic, governance and long-horizon scrutiny.

This working synthesis must be revisited after database execution, deduplication, screening and structured extraction. A source's presence here is not a final inclusion decision, and the seven principles are hypotheses for the review to test rather than conclusions the review has already established.
