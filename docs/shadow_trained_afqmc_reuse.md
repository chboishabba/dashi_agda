Primary attribution: Justin Provazza, Oliver Knitter and collaborators, *Shadow-trained neural quantum states*, [arXiv:2611.08129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08129), 2026. Earlier trapped-ion quantum-chemistry demonstration: [arXiv:2506.22408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22408). Related orbital-rotation shadows: [arXiv:2610.03103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03103). DASHI's source-owned code is not a reproduction or adaptation of authors' code.

## Reuse before construction

- `DASHI.Algebra.Quantum.DASHIStructuredStateCompression`: existing description/state, sound approximation, cost, and observability framework. Primary owner.
- `DASHI.Algebra.Quantum.FiniteQuantumRegister`, Shor finite-vector amplitude and Born weight/probability owners: computational-basis infrastructure. Shor outcome sampling does not itself provide chemistry shadows.
- `DASHI.Biology.MolecularMechanicsSamplingAlgorithms`: executable Metropolis, replica exchange and umbrella sampling on protein configurations. It is **not** phaseless auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo.
- `DASHI.Biology.BoltzmannConformationalEnsemble`: finite partition sums and Boltzmann probabilities, not a projector AFQMC measure.

`DASHI.Algebra.Quantum.ShadowTrainedAFQMCReuseExact` reuses the compression carrier and introduces *only* the missing problem-specific boundary: shadow measurements and trained descriptions, AFQMC walker-indexed overlaps, explicit fit certificates, separate local-energy stability certificates, and a finite walker-run projection. `DASHIQuantumComputationProgram` imports it into the quantum programme graph.

## Proved from supplied witnesses

Theorem `overlapBoundOnRun`: the per-walker overlap bound applies at every deterministically indexed walker generated by `FiniteWalkerRun`. The same trained description is used at every step.

Theorem `energyBoundOnRun`: a separately supplied pointwise local-energy stability bound applies at every such walker.

These theorems are total transport/reuse results. **No numeric tolerance, physical energy bound, quantum speedup, statistical coverage, or AFQMC accuracy is proved without a populated empirical source.** In particular, they do not derive energy stability from an overlap estimate; that inference can fail near nodes/small denominators and under phaseless constraints.

## Exact unfinished gates

1. **Original dataset**: obtain/reconstruct the 2025 16-qubit measured shadows and ideal-state comparison, measurement setting and noise calibration, with licenses and hashes. No original measurement arrays were available in this source audit.
2. **Numerical model**: implement the paper's symmetry-constrained, complex-amplitude neural-state training and the learned noise attenuation, then measure error out of sample.
3. **Physical AFQMC**: instantiate Slater determinant walkers, auxiliary-field Hubbard–Stratonovich propagation, importance weights, phaseless constraint, equilibration and population-control diagnostics. This is not achieved by renaming Metropolis.
4. **Error analysis**: give walker-distribution-dependent (rather than only all-walker) overlap, ratio, local-energy and accumulated estimator error with nonzero denominator/conditioning assumptions and independent validation.
5. **Replication and fairness**: use the same physical Hamiltonian/trial-state/orbital setup to compare original shadow processing, neural surrogate, and an optimized entirely classical trial state; report classical and quantum costs separately.
6. **Kernel certification**: run Agda 2.9 source checks on the new module and the quantum programme. No successful local compiler run or CI result has been obtained for this branch.

The paper's AWS $90,600 versus $596 figures represent reported estimated **classical** compute costs only; quantum machine time is excluded. These figures are source claims, not DASHI-reproduced measurements.

## 9 November 2026: importance-sampled phaseless finite-Hubbard cut

The new `scripts/qcafqmc_importance_phaseless_hubbard.py` goes beyond the earlier uniformly sampled HS paths. At every walker and step it computes all four candidate one-body HS evolutions, their complex overlaps against a selected trial state, and an overlap-guided **discrete categorical force-bias analogue**

```
q(field | walker) = |<trial | B(field) walker>| / sum_field' |<trial | B(field') walker>|
importance_factor = [p(field) / q(field | walker)] *
                    [<trial | B(field) walker> / <trial | walker>]
phaseless_factor = |importance_factor| * max(0, cos(arg(importance_factor)))
```

The walker is normalized to trial overlap 1 after each update. Systematic resampling controls the positive population. A same-Hamiltonian deterministic transfer matrix and exact diagonalization are independently available. `scripts/qcafqmc_importance_phaseless_check.py` tests four fixed seeds and a complex-trial negative control which discards the cosine. The code also records effective sample size.

**Important limit:** this is **finite discrete-field overlap-based importance sampling**, not a validated production molecular AFQMC force-bias from Cholesky- or density-fitted two-electron molecular integrals. In the real sign-free Hubbard case the phaseless cosine is typically trivial; a complex trial makes phase suppression visible but does not validate actual transition-metal phase bias or its thermodynamic/continuum error. This cannot be promoted to the paper's production implementation.

Local executable precursor tests (2,400 walkers, 8 steps, seeds 10–13, no branching) reported absolute energy errors 0.00818169, 0.01204027, 0.01551706, 0.00087013, mean 0.00915229, against the exactly propagated symmetric-Trotter mixed energy -0.53139847. For 300 walkers with complex trial, the phaseless-versus-deliberately-wrong magnitude-only estimator differed by 0.00068949. These are locally tested precursors to the committed equivalent scripts, **not exact-Git-commit run receipts**, and the chosen smoke acceptance bounds are not confidence intervals.

## 9 Nov: Maximum-cut proof strengthening — overlap conditioning and exact iPie CI weld

### P0 quantitative learned-trial error transport (new)

`scripts/qcafqmc_mixed_energy_error_certificate.py` supplies an executable instance of a mathematical upper bound for a learned trial's finite-state local energy. On the SAME H and walker, with D=<ideal|w>, N=<ideal|Hw>, d>=|Dlearn-D|, m>=|Nlearn-N| and |D|>d, the bound is

```
|Nlearn/Dlearn - N/D| <= (m*|D| + |N|*d) / (|D|*(|D|-d)).
```

The code obtains rigorous-in-exact-arithmetic conservative d and m from state-2norm error times the walker or H-walker norm, checks the Hermiticity and numerical domains, and **rejects near-zero overlaps** for which the denominator can cross zero. It makes no prediction about out-of-sample NQS generalization or accumulated phaseless projection bias. `scripts/qcafqmc_nqs_fci_error_audit.py` connects this exact inequality to the selected eight-orbital FCI eigenvector and trained NQS, after canonical global-phase alignment, using the actual same integral Hamiltonian.
