# Project Follow Through — pretest reliability sensitivity and follow-up discriminator

Independent research: Johl Brown (direction); OpenAI ChatGPT (AI-assisted audit). Original research credit belongs to the named authors below. No collaborative ESD work changed.

## Distinct original research roots
- Robert G. St. Pierre (1978), "Correcting Covariates for Unreliability: Does It Lead to Differences in an Evaluator's Conclusions?", *Evaluation Review* 2(3), 401–420, DOI 10.1177/0193841X7800200303. The abstract says the original national evaluation ran ANCOVA **with and without** correction of unreliable pretests; some conclusions changed. Evaluators reported both but based their conclusions on uncorrected pretests because the validity of reliability correction was disputed. This is original follow-on analysis of a subset of the evaluation data, not another independent trial.
- Wesley C. Becker and Russell Gersten (1982), "A Follow-up of Follow Through: The Later Effects of the Direct Instruction Model on Children in Fifth and Sixth Grades", *American Educational Research Journal* 19(1), DOI 10.3102/00028312019001075. Five-site follow-up of children who completed three years of the first–third-grade Direct Instruction programme. Its abstract reports strong significant decoding effects, consistent maths problem-solving and spelling, moderate effects elsewhere, and losses relative to the national norm after the programme ended. Site selection and comparability are separate questions. This is NOT the 1977 multi-model experiment, nor a randomised national durability test.
- Engelmann, Becker, Carnine and Gersten (1988), "The Direct Instruction Follow Through Model: Design and Outcomes", original article accessible through https://andymatuschak.org/files/1988-Engelmann.pdf. They acknowledge nonrandom student assignment, name Stanford Research Institute as data collector and Abt Associates as analyzer, and say findings were consistent over time and districts. They are original programme developers/advocates, not independent evaluators.
- Abt Associates (1977), volumes IV-A/B/C/D, maintain original evaluation authorship. House, Glass, McLean and Walker (1978) retain independent critique authorship; funding sources do not settle methodological validity.

## Original-transcript claims strengthened and limited
1. "Direct Instruction was best": retain score/domain/analysis/year distinction. Original effects should be reconstructed under both pretest-covariate reliability treatments before asserting ranking robustness. The 1978 St. Pierre finding that conclusions changed for *some models* is important sensitivity evidence; it does NOT demonstrate whether DI itself changed rank.
2. "Kids became confident as they acquired skills": affective-scale results alone do not identify competence as mediator. The 1982 follow-up evaluates academic outcomes, not this mediation claim.
3. "Implementation mattered": the 1982 follow-up measured durability at five selected sites, and distinguishes retained skills from continued achievement trajectory relative to national standardisation samples. It does not identify script adherence as cause.
4. "Government ignored effective results": adoption and dissemination remain separately sourced institutional questions; an academic follow-up cannot estimate the national policy counterfactual.

## Reproducibility target
For each 1977 model/domain/site/covariate specification, capture original contrast, pretest reliability estimate, adjustment formula, grayout criterion, analytic inclusion, estimate, uncertainty, data extraction method and source page. Represent outcomes under [uncorrected pretest] and [reliability-corrected pretest] as distinct specifications. Compare like with like, and do not invent unpublished cells.

For the 1982 five-site follow-up, record selection, matched-comparator construction, cohort, end-of-programme exposure, MAT/WRAT instrument, grade 5–6 outcome, school setting and whether the comparison is local cohort or national norms. Note differential attrition and nonrandom selection if reported.
