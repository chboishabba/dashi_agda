**This supersedes treating the 2022 Endangered OAG counterfactual as a promising independent approval-invalidity claim.** It does not supersede scientific/evidentiary critique of local koala viability or any independent source-authored QUT/QCC objections. No filing, notice, actual Court ruling or decision-maker reasoning is received or inferred.

## Primary statutory source and chronology

- Original [EPBC Act 1999, 1 July 2026 compilation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00485/2026-07-01/2026-07-01/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html), **section 158A**. Its definition **s158A(1)(k)** expressly covers a listed threatened species moving to a **higher endangerment category**; s158A(2) requires a **prior section 75 decision** and subsequent listing event; s158A(3) protects existing approval-process decisions; s158A(4) requires the intervening event to be **disregarded in later approval decisions** for the same action, notwithstanding other law by s158A(5). Section 158A was present well before these events.
- Original [Commonwealth Policy Statement, Listing Events under the EPBC Act (December 2010)](https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/epbc-act-policy-listing-events.pdf) independently explains the s158A consequence for post-s75 uplisting and identifies section 133 approvals/section 134 conditions as approval-process decisions.
- **EPBC 2019/8575:** Saunders Havill's January 2026 original PD states Commonwealth controlled-action determination dated **9 April 2020**, under ss18/18A; the Commonwealth's original 2026 [draft preliminary document public notice](https://epbcpublicportal.environment.gov.au/_entity/sharepointdocumentlocation/4f8edc14-9ffb-ee11-9f89-00224892a860/2ab10dab-d681-4911-b881-cc99413f07b6?file=2019-8575-Draft-PD.pdf) independently confirms the action had been declared controlled, but the **dated original section 75 instrument itself is not in hand**.
- Official [DCCEEW endangered koala listing](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/biodiversity/threatened/species/koalas/listing-under-national-environmental-law): **12 February 2022** Queensland/NSW/ACT uplisting from Vulnerable.
- User-supplied original approved Part 9 EPBC **2019/8575** is dated **16 October 2026**, signed Mark Hall (not 15 October).

Chronology reported: **2020-04-09 s75 → 2022-02-12 uplisting → 2026-10-16 signed approval**. Section 158A is an apparently direct and substantial statutory answer to an argument that the later uplisting, **by itself**, legally required the later approval decision to switch species category.

## Effect on the mathematical work

The reconstructed koala OAG under the **proponent's original Vulnerable 0.2%** annual extinction factor is **108.3451326%**. The counterfactual **Endangered 1.2%** factor gives **88.8287572%**, but this remains a **conditional conservation-risk/sensitivity calculation**, NOT a demonstrable EPBC approval defect or proof that the delegate was legally required to use the 1.2% category. Earlier loose wording suggesting otherwise is superseded.

The stronger live 2012-policy and ecological challenges are independently sourced:

1. Is the underlying accepted **quality improvement** scientifically credible? Official National EPA current OAG guidance says in most cases an above-one-point gain requires **strong scientific evidence**, and the 2012 original guide §2G requires credible confidence about a large future change and delivery track record.
2. Proponent A16 reports **MWW AU1 3.40→6.86** and AU2 **3.44→6.71** but uses rounded **3→7** in the OAG, changing isolated source-conditional total from **108.3451% to 99.9918%** if only those two unrounded changes are substituted. Whether such rounding is **allowed and actually accepted** requires original workbook, not accusation.
3. QUT Taylor/Maguire/Lewis and QCC Gough 12 March 2026 originals challenge mature forest/corridor/heat refuge and habitat function. Exact source 56-AU OAG percentages cannot replace these empirical attributes, even if correctly computed.
4. The signed Part 9 conditions and original 95B/95C and registered offset records remain separate statutory/physical proof gates. S158A does not establish that clearing, an offset declaration, breach or filed action occurred.

**S158A cannot be repurposed to silence different factual environmental evidence merely because it concerns a koala.** The statute concerns the *listing event* itself, and any other independent assessment obligation or distinct actual cause would require its own exact legal analysis.
