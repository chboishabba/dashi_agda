## Narrow theorem
Two possible instructional histories can have the same academic assessment projection while differing in learner agency. Thus there exists no function from the single academic surface to the agency surface reproducing both histories. This does NOT refute Direct Instruction, deny its academic effects, establish a benefit from agency, or estimate programme-level heterogeneity.

## Discriminators before transfer
1. Programme assignment vs delivered instruction; site, cohort, year, follow-up horizon.
2. Reading/math gains vs affective outcomes vs independently elicited agency.
3. Nominal service availability vs accessible participation vs recognised authority.
4. Self-image instrument score vs learner's ability to contest interpretation or shape future tasks.
5. Mechanism fidelity vs script literalism; adaptation effects require identified intervention contrasts.
6. Digital ESD competence claims require prospective design, learner/community governance, and externality accounting; no automatic transport from 1970s US schoolchildren to Australian higher education.
