## R1 — 2024 original journal paper cites a different kind of study for its '60%' Rodin/Helmholtz statement

**Claim in original author source:** A. D. Ramírez-Galindo, H. A. Pérez-Olivas, G. Basurto-Islas, A. Hernández-Rayas, F. González López and T. Córdova-Fraga, *Vortex magnetic induction: Mathematical, geometric and experimental characterization*, *Revista Mexicana de Física* **70**, 030901 (**May–June 2024 issue**, original Received **17 Feb 2023** / Accepted **27 Dec 2023**; SciELO online carrier **22 Sep 2025**). **Original PDF inspected page 5**, [download](https://rmf.smf.mx/ojs/index.php/rmf/article/download/6973/6911/27256), DOI 10.31349/RevMexFis.70.030901. On page 5, Discussion, the authors state that when a Rodin coil is compared with a uniform-field system such as Helmholtz, the effect is enhanced by **up to 60%**, citing numbered **[24]**. The paper's abstract separately presents the comparison as if it underlies the article's result.

**What [24] actually is:** J. Saikia, G. Pandey, S. Sasidharan, F. Antony, H. B. Nemade, S. Kumar, N. Chaudhary and V. Ramakrishnan, '[Electric Field Disruption of Amyloid Aggregation: Potential Noninvasive Therapy for Alzheimer’s Disease](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acschemneuro.8b00490)', *ACS Chemical Neuroscience* **10** (2019), 2250–2262. The original **publisher-authored abstract and bibliographic metadata** (online **15 Feb 2019**, DOI **10.1021/acschemneuro.8b00490**, PMID 30707008) were inspected. This distinct **IIT Guwahati author group** investigates **external electric and magnetic fields affecting amyloid peptide aggregation in vitro**, and reports **15–38% reduced amyloid-related toxicity for electric-field-treated peptides** versus untreated peptide controls in cell-line assays. The abstract **does not describe** a named Rodin-coil versus Helmholtz-coil matched comparison or a 60%-larger benefit for a Rodin coil. The journal full article is **not freely read here**; it is not legitimate to categorically assert that the full paper *cannot* contain some relevant comparison.

**Assessment:** exact `[24]→Saikia-2019` citation is paid. Support for the **specified Rodin-versus-Helmholtz 60% figure is not paid by the available cited abstract**, and the 2024 original paper's Results section reports numerical comparison of **theoretical / simulated / mapped B fields for a Rodin geometry**, not a direct two-coil biological intervention trial with a Helmholtz matched-field control in that 2024 experiment. This is a **proposition-specific support gap**, not a judgment about article integrity or authors' motives. Preserve what the authors *say* and what the cited original actually discusses separately. Journal webpage and printed PDF are one article, not two independent measurements. The Saikia paper is an independent biological research root but *different field-intervention geometry*, and any claimed biomedical benefits remain in vitro, not Alzheimer's treatment.

**Science-interpretation corollary:** the 2024 authors discuss a causal theory about greater magnetic **field gradients** explaining biological differences. A gradient `∇B` or Jacobian `∂B_i/∂x_j` is conceptually different from merely reporting `B=∇×A` (curl of a vector potential) or drawing three-dimensional direction maps of `B`. The original PDF calls its `B=∇×A` expression (its equation 7) a field-gradient computation, which does not alone supply measured `∂B_i/∂x_j` or a matched-dose gradient causal comparison. An ordinary nonuniform magnetic field is not a novel 3-6-9 or quantum ontology. We do not recompute their exact field model or make a kernel-certified judgment in this documentary cut.

The 2013 H. Pérez RSI title/DOI was **not identified among the 2024 original paper's 24 numbered references**. That finding matters for explicit bibliography-based independence and discovery, **not** as proof of intentional omission or an incorrect scientific conclusion.
