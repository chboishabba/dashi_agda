## 1. New first-party original (rather than one more inherited assertion)

**Pandey, G.; Saikia, J.; Sasidharan, S.; Joshi, D. C.; Thota, S.; Nemade, H. B.; Chaudhary, N.; Ramakrishnan, V.** (2 June 2017), *Modulation of Peptide Based Nano-Assemblies with Electric and Magnetic Fields*, **Scientific Reports** 7, 2726, DOI [10.1038/s41598-017-02609-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-02609-z). [Original publisher's full text](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-017-02609-z), distinct [NIH PubMed Central original-paper archive](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5457452/) (same work, NOT replication).

Original paper **Methods and Results inspected** in openly available full HTML, not inference from social quotations. Under the authors' stated conditions, **diphenylalanine FF** at 2mg/mL cools **95°C → 25°C over five hours**. Experimental conditions include AC electric **150 or 300 V/cm at 50 Hz**, corresponding DC electric fields, **0.6 T magnetic field** and no applied field control. The authors report that electric-field conditions markedly inhibit assembly during exposure, while the separately treated 0.6 T magnetic sample and control exhibit visible aggregates. Original context is peptide nanoassembly/physical morphology, not Rodin/Helmholtz device-comparison efficacy; the 0.6 T condition is **600 mT**, not the **1–2 mT** levels mentioned for other separate studies in the 2024 Rodin Discussion. One paper's response is not a clinical treatment trial.
