**Seven selected docket-document objects**, not seven independent institutions, six new agency confirmations or seven verified PDF audits. There is ONE institutional Senate publisher, with three separate parliamentary inquiries. The same named producer's new original submission to another inquiry is a distinct *file* and a repeat producer, not automatically independent research. Specific original PDF face/byte inspections of these selected filings were NOT accomplished here because APH PDF fetch returned HTTP 403/timeout. The official search index did expose the text of HPPL's 13 October submission, but that is a searchable text rendition, not an independently hashed/visually certified original PDF. Never claim same-text identity, identical hashes, equal authors or a changed position without checking bytes.

**Time-based negative discriminators:** Neither the August submissions nor October ones can have caused drafting of the *February* bill; the **13 October** HPPL submission cannot have caused initial introduction of the *28 September* government bill. They could have contributed to amendments, political debate, other legislation or later campaigns—subject to actual same-object proof. The February committee list only establishes no HPPL/IPA filing among its *six published entries*; it does not establish there was no lobbying, private conversation or unlisted correspondence.

## Positive evidence: real third-party research reuse without inferred financing

[Original November 2022 Senate Community Affairs report, chapter 2, §§2.15–2.16](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Community_Affairs/WorkforceIncentive2022/Report/section?id=committees%2Freportsen%2F024985%2F80109) reports that **the Institute of Public Affairs explicitly referred to a National Seniors Australia survey** about pensioner employment disincentives, giving a 20% participation statement and approximately 510,000 potential pensioners. This is a concrete, public, producer-attributed **research claim → institutional citation** join.

That is distinct from:

- **National Seniors → Deloitte**, an original letter naming the client and modelling contractor: genuine commissioned modelling, but its forecast remains conditional and causal adoption unproven ([original National Seniors-hosted Deloitte letter](https://nationalseniors.com.au/uploads/NSA-Let-Pensioners-Work-Age-Pension-Modelling-FINAL-14112022.pdf)).
- **HPPL → IPA**, where a NSW court reported historic aggregate contributions for 2016/17. That supports a past financial relationship, NOT an original purchase order or editorial instruction commissioning the *2022 pension-work submission*.
- **NSA → IPA survey citation**, which proves neither joint authorship nor an independent statistical replication; parliamentary report is one report quoting a submitter and source of the survey. It is NOT evidence that HPPL ordered the citation.
- **One Nation 2025 original policy**, which is a further publisher's electoral proposal, and Hanson's retrospective 2026 Rinehart-origin statement, which is a distinct first-person attribution rather than a pre-2025 original instruction.

The same [Committee report, §§2.27–2.31](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Committees/Senate/Community_Affairs/WorkforceIncentive2022/Report/section?id=committees%2Freportsen%2F024985%2F80109) also reports specific *different* proposals: ACCI **$15,600 annually**; VCCI **$10,000** increase; National Seniors **$6,000 biannually**; IPA argued the government increase was too small; HPPL's 13 October primary indexed submission called for **unlimited** Work Bonus and removal of Centrelink reporting ([indexed original PDF location](https://www.aph.gov.au/DocumentStore.ashx?id=7596775e-a631-40bd-be57-abe6b5efd5a1&subId=722612)). This is concrete institutional diversity, not confirmation of one authored scheme.

**The Senate committee recommended the narrower government bill**, and the government enacted the temporary Work Bonus increase, subject to its original statute and commencement stages. Recommendation, assent and measurable employment effects are three different kinds of object. The Senate report explicitly suggested monitoring—NOT a completed effectiveness evaluation.
