## First-party paper claims: Tucker 1977
**Visually inspected original PDF page index 5 (printed p4):** Tucker states the original $120m fiscal 1968 programme request, **$15m** final allocation, **$2.8m** OEO advance for 40 pilot districts and subsequent $2.78m repayment, and $70m cumulatively furnished by 1970 across 173 sites. These are *Tucker's contemporaneous reported figures*, not independently audited federal appropriations.

Paper's author warning on its title leaf: opinions solely author's, not official USOE position. Therefore do **not** type the source as an official USOE adoption/refusal order. Tucker discusses programme social-service expectations, parental role, experimentation, politics and funding. His reported initial funding constraint precedes the 1977 results and cannot itself be evidence of retaliatory defunding of DI.

Bank Street College Archives holds the original Tucker AERA item as **RG11, Box 12, bsc.11.01.D.149**, https://bankstreetarchives.libraryhost.com/repositories/2/archival_objects/9955 . Another original same-root custody channel, not an independent witness.

## First-party retrospective claims: Evans 1981
**Visually inspected original PDF page index 4 (printed p4):** Evans explains Follow Through began as a much larger service proposal, the original $15m funding proved insufficient for intended coverage, and administrators therefore converted the initiative to research-and-development/planned variation. Evans explicitly identifies conflict between an R&D experiment and stakeholders' social-action/service expectations. He treats the shift to research as sensible while criticising the subsequent mismatched governance. ERIC's own summary notes Evans favours explicit programme goals, randomized/quasi-experimental designs, sufficient replications and treating implementation as essential. These are Evans's original retrospective historical interpretations, NOT an archival record showing a specific official's decision to suppress results. Evans is not an independent classroom efficacy trial.

Evans's original paper says **17 models in the planned-variation R&D thrust**, offering an original author's contextual use of 17 independent of the later annotated bibliography; do not conflate with House's 13 evaluated subset or all approaches piloted historically.

## Severe, source-relative interpretation
H1 (strong institutional-mismatch mechanism): Initial funding under-fulfilment + research/service expectations predate the 1977 results. Tucker 1977 and Evans 1981 provide contemporaneous/participant-perspective support, with different authors/dates. Specific legislative acts, federal allocation ledgers and administrative decisions still needed for external corroboration.

H2 (strong later policy criticism): Despite positive DI results in the published Abt analysis, later dissemination/funding policy may not have used performance-maximisation alone. To adjudicate, obtain dated programme-by-model appropriation and adoption rules **after** 15 April 1977. Initial FY1968 appropriations are an *invalid comparator* to a 1977 evidence response.

H3 (intentional cover-up): APM's archival reporting regarding preliminary findings remains a source-reported documentary claim, distinct from Tucker/Evans funding descriptions. Need original nondisclosure instructions/decision authority and publication chronology; the 1977 final evaluation was published.

H4 (school-level effect): Tucker/Evans research/service governance helps explain implementation and transport, but cannot replace original Abt model×site×cohort×instrument estimates, House and St Pierre's exact reanalysis or longitudinal follow-up.
