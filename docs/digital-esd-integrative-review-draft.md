# Digital Education for Sustainability, and the Sustainability of Digital Education

## A Reciprocal Framework for Transformative Education and Sustainable Development

**Manuscript status:** working integrative-conceptual-review draft. The conceptual framework and methodology are specified; the declared Scopus, Web of Science, ERIC, ACM Digital Library and IEEE Xplore searches have not yet been executed, so no database-complete corpus, screening counts or review findings are claimed here.

## Abstract

Digital transformation and Education for Sustainable Development (ESD) are increasingly treated as linked agendas, yet the relationship is often framed in only one direction: digital technologies are considered as tools for improving sustainability learning. This paper develops a reciprocal framework in which digital education can build ESD capacity while sustainability simultaneously constrains the design, procurement, governance and evaluation of digital education itself. We use an integrative conceptual review with a transparent structured-search protocol. The review is designed to retain distinctions among pedagogical effectiveness, educational transformation, participant epistemic agency, institutional change, infrastructure lifecycle impacts, circularity, interoperability and longitudinal durability. Primary institutional frameworks and evaluations are interpreted within their stated source roles rather than promoted into intervention-level evidence. The synthesis therefore asks both what digital innovation can contribute to ESD and under what environmental, social, economic, epistemic and intergenerational conditions digital education should itself be regarded as sustainable. The paper contributes a consumer-relative evidence framework for distinguishing technology adoption from transformation, task performance from learning, sustainability content from sustainable infrastructure, and source acquisition from evidence closure.

## 1. Introduction

Education systems are being asked to respond simultaneously to two large transitions: the transition toward more sustainable societies and the rapid diffusion of digital and artificial-intelligence technologies. UNESCO's ESD for 2030 roadmap places transformation at the centre of Education for Sustainable Development, with priority action areas spanning policy, learning environments, educator capacity, youth and local action. The framework therefore concerns change in educational systems and practices, not simply the addition of sustainability content to existing instruction.

This system-level distinction matters empirically. UNESCO's 2026 mid-term evaluation of ESD for 2030 reported substantial implementation activity across the framework while also concluding that systemic transformation remained limited and recommending greater strategic coherence, monitoring and national ownership. The coexistence of activity with limited transformation motivates a central distinction in this review: implementation activity, technology uptake or programme visibility cannot be treated as sufficient evidence of educational-system transformation.

A parallel distinction is now prominent in digital education. The OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026 synthesises emerging evidence on generative AI and argues that successful task performance with digital tools does not automatically imply learning. Pedagogical guidance, human-centred design, research, governance and enabling infrastructure remain material conditions. This reinforces an older education-technology lesson: access to technology and the presence of a digital tool are not themselves educational outcomes.

The green and digital agendas are also not automatically synergistic. UNESCO's 2024 paper on bridging the green and digital transitions through education explicitly frames them as transitions that may be perceived as distinct or in tension, while proposing ways education can bring them into alignment. This creates an important research opportunity. The question is not only how digital technologies can support sustainability learning, but also how sustainability principles should govern digital education itself.

That reverse direction is increasingly concrete. The 2026 UNESCO-UNICEF-ITU Charter for Public Digital Learning Platforms frames public digital learning platforms as components of education systems and identifies public accountability, inclusion, pedagogical purpose, complementarity, openness, interoperability and trustworthiness as design and governance principles. In parallel, ITU-T L.1410 provides a methodology for life-cycle assessment of ICT goods, networks and services, including comparison with a reference product system, while ITU-T L.1023 provides a circularity scoring method covering durability, repair, reuse, recycling and upgradeability. These sources make it possible to move beyond generic claims that digital education should be 'green' toward explicit lifecycle, circularity and infrastructure coordinates.

We therefore frame digital education and ESD as a reciprocal relation:

\[
\text{Digital education} \rightleftarrows \text{Education for Sustainable Development}.
\]

The forward direction concerns the contribution of digital education to ESD capability. The reverse direction concerns the sustainability conditions imposed on digital education itself. The paper's working conceptual claim is that transformative digital ESD requires both:

\[
\text{TransformativeDigitalESD}
=
\text{ESDCapacity}
\times
\text{SustainabilityAdmissibility}.
\]

This is a conceptual decomposition rather than an empirical effect equation. Its purpose is to prevent evidence from one side of the relation—for example a learning gain—from silently paying a different question such as infrastructure lifecycle sustainability or participant governance.

## 2. Review aims and research questions

The review has five research questions.

1. **ESD capacity.** Through what pedagogical, curricular, competence, learning-environment and institutional mechanisms can digital education build ESD capacity?
2. **Transformation.** Under what conditions does digital innovation support educational or system transformation rather than technology adoption or task performance alone?
3. **Reflexive sustainability.** How should environmental, social, economic, lifecycle, circularity and infrastructure considerations constrain digital education itself?
4. **Participant governance.** How should learner and participant voice, epistemic agency and governance enter digital-ESD design without treating literature, consent or consultation as equivalent to local constitutive authority?
5. **Durability and time.** What evidence and conditions bear on durability, institutionalisation, context transfer and longitudinal transformation, and which claims remain future empirical debt?

## 3. Conceptual framework

### 3.1 Digital education as an enabler of ESD

The first strand treats digital systems as possible pedagogical and institutional enablers. Relevant coordinates include pedagogy, curriculum, learning environment, competence development, institutional practice, professional development and policy. Evidence in this strand is not reduced to tool presence. A digital platform, AI assistant or online environment can be present without producing the same pedagogical, learning or institutional outcome in every implementation.

### 3.2 Sustainability as a constraint on digital education

The second strand reverses the observation direction. Digital technologies become sustainability objects whose environmental, social, economic and governance characteristics require evaluation. Relevant coordinates include energy and emissions, hardware manufacture, water and e-waste where applicable, service life, repairability, reuse and upgradeability, interoperability, migration and export, procurement/support conditions, accessibility, equity, public accountability and data governance.

This reverse strand intentionally separates two propositions:

\[
\text{technology used to teach sustainability}
\neq
\text{sustainable technology}.
\]

A study can provide evidence that a digital intervention supports sustainability awareness while providing no lifecycle evidence about the devices, networks, data centres or replacement cycles that enable it.

### 3.3 Transformation rather than adoption

The framework distinguishes technology adoption, learning or performance change, and institutional/system transformation. These states may be related, but they are not interchangeable. UNESCO's 2026 ESD mid-term evaluation provides a programme-level illustration of why implementation activity and system transformation should remain separate coordinates. The OECD's 2026 Digital Education Outlook supplies an analogous learning-level distinction between successful task performance and learning.

### 3.4 Epistemic agency and participant governance

Participant engagement is retained as a separate evidence and governance dimension. Participatory ESD, student voice and co-design literature can inform the method, but a prior study cannot create authority for a new participant population. For transfer claims, contextual generalisation and same-target-context participant-authority evidence are treated as conjunctive obligations. Procedural ethics and consent remain necessary where applicable but are not treated as substitutes for constitutive epistemic agency.

### 3.5 Time, durability and intergenerational option preservation

Short-term learning outcomes do not determine durable institutional transformation. The review therefore extracts time horizon explicitly and distinguishes immediate performance, learning outcomes, behavioural outcomes, institutionalisation and longer-term system effects. Intergenerational framing is used as an option-preservation and consequence-horizon constraint; it is not used to manufacture cultural authority from citation.

## 4. Methods

### 4.1 Review design

The paper is designed as an **integrative conceptual review with a transparent structured search**. It is not labelled a systematic review, and it is not an empirical intervention study. The design follows the broad integrative-review logic described by Whittemore and Knafl: problem identification, literature search, data evaluation, data analysis/synthesis and presentation. SANRA is retained as a reporting-quality comparator for narrative review features, not as a universal evidence-ranking instrument. PRISMA-S is used to strengthen transparent reporting of information sources and search execution, and PRESS informs the construction and review of database search strategies. Citation of these methods does not itself constitute method execution.

### 4.2 Information sources

The declared bibliographic surfaces are:

- Scopus;
- Web of Science;
- ERIC;
- ACM Digital Library; and
- IEEE Xplore.

Open-web searching and source snowballing are used for discovery of primary institutional documents, standards and candidate literature, but are not treated as substitutes for the declared database searches.

At the date of this working draft, the five database searches, deduplication, eligibility screening and structured extraction remain unexecuted. Their eventual execution records will retain the exact query, platform or interface where applicable, execution date, result count and result export so that the search lineage can be audited.

### 4.3 Search strategy

The search is organised around six concept families already represented in the formal review ledger:

1. digital education and ESD;
2. reflexive digital sustainability;
3. lifecycle and circularity;
4. participant agency and governance;
5. longitudinal and institutional impact; and
6. open interoperability and repairability.

A platform-neutral concept vocabulary will combine terms such as **digital education**, **educational technology**, **online learning**, **generative AI**, **education for sustainable development**, **sustainability**, **life cycle assessment**, **energy**, **carbon**, **water**, **e-waste**, **circularity**, **repairability**, **interoperability**, **student voice**, **learner agency**, **participatory research**, **co-design**, **longitudinal**, **institutionalisation** and **durability**. Platform-specific syntax, controlled vocabulary, field codes, proximity operators, spelling variants, filters and date restrictions will be translated separately and retained verbatim in each database execution receipt rather than reconstructed after screening.

### 4.4 Eligibility principles

Because this is an integrative review, admissible evidence can include empirical studies, systematic or scoping reviews, methodological studies, standards, institutional evaluations and policy/framework documents. Inclusion is role-relative rather than source-type-blind. A source must contribute to at least one declared research question and must retain enough information to identify its evidence or authority role.

For every included source, the synthesis will retain:

- source identity and source kind;
- publication date;
- population, education level or system context where relevant;
- jurisdiction or institutional context where relevant;
- digital technology or practice under study;
- sustainability dimension(s);
- study, review, standard or policy design;
- outcome or claim type;
- time horizon;
- lifecycle/circularity boundary where applicable;
- participant-agency or authority role where applicable;
- interoperability/governance coordinates where applicable;
- same-object versus contextual status;
- context-transfer limitations; and
- uncertainty, limitations and defeaters.

Acquisition alone does not create inclusion. Search hits, snippets, standards and institutional reports are not flattened into a single evidence class.

### 4.5 Exclusion and non-promotion rules

Records will be excluded from a research-question synthesis when they do not address the relevant consumer or when the available source does not support the claimed relationship. More importantly, several forms of non-promotion are enforced even for retained sources:

- technology presence does not establish educational transformation;
- task performance does not establish learning;
- learning gain does not establish system transformation;
- technology used for sustainability education does not establish sustainability of the technology;
- a lifecycle or circularity method does not create a deployment-specific inventory;
- a standard or charter does not prove implementation or durability of a deployed system;
- participation literature, consultation or consent does not create local participant authority;
- short-term outcomes do not establish longitudinal durability;
- citation does not import proof or authority; and
- source acquisition does not equal manuscript inclusion or evidence-synthesis closure.

### 4.6 Data extraction

Structured extraction will use an 18-coordinate schema corresponding to the fields above. The extraction object will retain the included-set identity produced by screening and will produce a source-role/scope matrix. Downstream synthesis therefore depends on the exact screened corpus rather than on a manually curated bibliography that has lost its search genealogy.

### 4.7 Synthesis

The review will use a structured integrative synthesis rather than statistical meta-analysis across heterogeneous source roles. Each synthesis cell is indexed by:

- research question;
- evidence role;
- reciprocity direction;
- transformation status;
- evidence-payment status;
- source reference;
- population/context;
- time horizon; and
- limitation/transfer boundary.

Three transformation states are distinguished: technology adoption only; learning/performance change; and institutional/system transformation, with an explicit unresolved state where evidence is insufficient. Evidence status is likewise separated into source-role payment, contextual-coordinate payment, same-object payment and explicit residual/unpaid dependency.

This structure permits primary standards and institutional frameworks to inform design or interpretation without being counted as intervention-effect studies, while empirical studies can contribute outcome evidence without silently acquiring normative or authority roles they do not possess.

### 4.8 Lifecycle and circularity interpretation

ICT lifecycle evidence is interpreted against ITU-T L.1410, which separates ICT life-cycle assessment from comparative analysis against a reference product system. Circularity coordinates are informed by ITU-T L.1023, including durability and the ability to repair, reuse, recycle and upgrade. These methods define measurement coordinates; they do not provide an inventory for any digital-education intervention unless same-object deployment data are actually acquired.

### 4.9 Participant governance and context transfer

Participant-governance claims are synthesised with a stricter transfer boundary than ordinary literature relevance. Evidence from prior populations can inform candidate mechanisms and study design, but a claim of constitutive epistemic agency in a new target context requires context-generalisation evidence together with evidence of participant authority in that target context. The current conceptual review does not claim to have performed such a local participant-authority study.

### 4.10 Primary institutional sources and source roles

The review currently retains the following primary institutional sources as source-bounded inputs:

- UNESCO, *Education for sustainable development: a roadmap* (2020): ESD-for-2030 implementation framework and system-transformation context;
- UNESCO, *Mid-term evaluation of the ESD for 2030 framework, 2021–2024* (2026): programme-level implementation and transformation evidence;
- UNESCO, *Bridging the green and digital transitions through education* (2024): green/digital twin-transition antecedent and tension/alignment framing;
- OECD, *OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026: Exploring Effective Uses of Generative AI in Education* (2026), DOI 10.1787/062a7394-en: current digital-education evidence and policy synthesis, including the performance-versus-learning distinction;
- UNESCO-UNICEF-ITU, *Charter for Public Digital Learning Platforms* (2026): normative infrastructure/governance principles including public accountability, openness, interoperability and trustworthiness;
- ITU-T L.1410 (11/2024): ICT life-cycle assessment methodology; and
- ITU-T L.1023 (08/2023): ICT circularity scoring methodology.

Each source retains its institutional, methodological, normative or evidentiary role. None is promoted into a different role simply because it is authoritative within its own domain.

### 4.11 Methodological status and reproducibility

The formal repository represents search and synthesis as dependent receipts. Database exports feed deduplication; the exact deduplicated object feeds screening; the exact screening object feeds structured extraction; and the extraction object feeds the source/scope matrix used in synthesis. This design prevents downstream claims from being marked complete merely because upstream work is described as completed in prose.

The current draft is therefore reproducible at the level of declared method and schema but not yet at the level of completed database execution. No unobserved database receipt, screening count or extracted corpus is reported.

## 5. Planned synthesis structure

The completed evidence synthesis will be organised around five coupled sections rather than around technologies alone:

1. **Digital education as an ESD-capacity mechanism** — pedagogy, curriculum, learning environments, competencies and institutional practice;
2. **From adoption to transformation** — conditions separating tool presence, performance/learning outcomes and system change;
3. **The sustainability of digital education** — lifecycle, circularity, energy/material infrastructure, repairability, interoperability and public digital infrastructure;
4. **Participant epistemic agency and governance** — voice, co-design, authority, context transfer and institutional choice; and
5. **Durability, institutionalisation and intergenerational consequences** — longitudinal evidence, support windows, persistence, option preservation and residual empirical debt.

## 6. Current evidence-status boundary

The repository currently contains a broad open-web and primary-source snowball, source-role attribution, lifecycle/circularity method acquisition, participant-governance transfer formalisation and the reciprocal conceptual framework. Those materials are **candidate inputs**, not a completed review corpus.

The following remain operationally unpaid at this draft stage:

- execution of the five declared bibliographic database searches;
- retained database exports and counts;
- cross-database deduplication;
- eligibility screening with exclusion reasons;
- structured extraction of the included corpus; and
- corpus-dependent evidence synthesis.

Consequently, this draft does not report review-result counts, pooled effects, prevalence estimates or claims of evidence completeness.
