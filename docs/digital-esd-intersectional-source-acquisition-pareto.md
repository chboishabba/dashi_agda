# Digital-ESD Intersectional Source-Acquisition Pareto

Date: 2026-09-17
Branch: `agent/digital-esd-paper-methodology-primary-sources`

This is an acquisition/provenance ledger, not the final included-study corpus. Search execution, deduplication, eligibility screening, structured extraction and `SourceAuditAdmission` remain mandatory before any candidate contributes to final synthesis.

## Selection rule

Prioritise sources that pay several currently weak situated/intersectional fibres at once while preserving observer identity and source scope.

Do not optimise for citation count or generic topical similarity. In particular:

```text
Pareto priority != source authority
candidate acquisition != included study
identifier completeness != evidence completeness
broad equity language != intersectional interaction evidence
participant mentioned != participant authority
```

QID and Dewey coordinates remain unresolved unless independently verified; nearest-label substitution is prohibited.

## P0 intersectional frontier

### Krazinski & Foley (2024)

- DOI: `10.1163/25888803-bja10026`
- Source: *Intersections of Marginalization and Possibility: A Phenomenological Analysis of Disabled Students' Experiences with Online Learning*
- Targets:
  - disabled-student situated voice;
  - disability × race/gender/class interaction;
  - support/agency and the absence of disabled students from normative schooling design.
- Ceiling: qualitative phenomenological study; no prevalence or universal causal effect.

### Marsden, Munn, Magee, Ferrinda, St. Pierre & Third (2025)

- DOI: `10.1007/s10639-024-13012-3`
- Source: *Inclusive online learning in Australia: Barriers and enablers*
- Targets:
  - affordability / credit rating / housing security;
  - language;
  - connectivity/device provisioning;
  - teacher/family digital literacy;
  - individual × interpersonal × organisational × infrastructural interaction.
- Ceiling: three-school Western Australian study; no national prevalence claim.

### McNicholl, Desmond & Gallagher (2026)

- DOI: `10.1080/17483107.2026.2623463`
- PMID: `41631993`
- Source: *Learnings from assistive technology use in an online, higher education environment*
- Targets:
  - disability/access × assistive-technology continuity;
  - inclusive support systems;
  - collaborative spaces;
  - co-design / disabled participant decision voice.
- Ceiling: qualitative `n=13`, Ireland HE; not effectiveness evidence for a named AT product.

### Rajapakshe et al. (2026)

- DOI: `10.1016/j.ssaho.2026.102621`
- Source: *Accessibility and usability of virtual learning platforms: Lived experiences of visually impaired undergraduates in Sri Lanka*
- Targets:
  - visual impairment × interface usability;
  - screen-reader compatibility;
  - institutional support;
  - peer connection/social isolation;
  - situated student agency.
- Ceiling: qualitative `n=15`, Sri Lankan HE; no universal platform ranking.

### Gaudeau et al. (2026)

- DOI: `10.18653/v1/2026.bea-1.50`
- Distinct preprint identity: arXiv `2606.13691`
- Source: *Incentives Of EdTech: A Systematic Review Of EduNLP Research*
- Targets:
  - who is represented as beneficiary/stakeholder;
  - teacher under-representation;
  - private-sector incentive × educational-infrastructure need;
  - real-world deployment gap;
  - ethical acknowledgement versus action.
- Ceiling: systematic review of its bounded ACL/EduNLP corpus; no automatic generalisation to all EdTech.

### Kenney et al. (2022)

- DOI: `10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.29514`
- Source: *Costs, Reach, and Benefits of COVID-19 Pandemic Electronic Benefit Transfer and Grab-and-Go School Meals for Ensuring Youths' Access to Food During School Closures*
- Targets:
  - school-site loss × meal provisioning continuity;
  - low-income eligibility and programme reach;
  - public implementation cost;
  - family time/cost burden.
- Ceiling: US pandemic school-closure economic evaluation; does not imply online education universally causes food insecurity.

## P1 context expansion

### Ma, Cui & Zhou (2026)

- DOI: `10.1016/j.techsoc.2026.103280`
- 11,501 undergraduates across 28 Chinese research universities.
- Targets family-background disparities and school/family/individual obstacles.
- Useful large-sample comparator after situated P0 studies; perceived effectiveness is not causal identification.

### Ali, Prompiengchai & Joordens (2024)

- DOI: `10.3390/standards4010001`
- EdTech procurement environmental scan across Canadian post-secondary institutions.
- Targets standards use, accessibility/privacy/data-care criteria and procurement centralisation.
- Procurement actors are not substitutes for student/disabled participant authority.

## Round 2 residual frontier

### Werse (2023)

- DOI: `10.1111/bjet.13327`
- Targets educational-technology lifecycle × global inequity in production/disposal.
- Conceptual ecojustice source only; not a deployment LCI/LCA.

### Sheikh, Stolberg & Gilmour (2025)

- DOI: `10.1177/00420859241279446`
- Systematic review of 31 studies of advanced school surveillance.
- Targets surveillance/security × poor/minoritized student incidence and perceived safety.
- Review evidence does not establish universal effects for a named monitoring product.

### Sankoh, Hogle, Payton & Ledbetter (2023)

- DOI: `10.3389/feduc.2023.1053590`
- Nine-parent phenomenological study in Manitoba.
- Targets remote delivery × disabled/special-needs child × shifted household teaching/care burden and parent involvement in planning.
- Parent observer is not disabled-student voice.

### Kelso, Soneji, Rahaman, Shoshitaishvili & Hasan (2024)

- DOI: `10.1145/3658644.3690353`
- Semi-structured interviews with 13 EdTech leaders across seven HEIs.
- Targets vendor visibility/power asymmetry × privacy/security/accountability in acquisition.
- Institutional observer cannot manufacture student consent or lived privacy impact.

## Next residuals after these two rounds

The remaining high-value searches are now narrower:

1. environmental/device lifecycle evidence that directly identifies affected worker/community populations rather than only aggregate footprint;
2. disability/accessibility × affordability with direct joint measurement rather than parallel discussion;
3. AI/learning-analytics governance × participant decision authority with actual student/teacher co-governance evidence;
4. digital service continuity × accessibility after upgrades/migrations/vendor failure;
5. school social provisioning beyond meals: counselling, safe space, peer connection, disability support and informal care under remote/hybrid substitution;
6. sources where Indigenous/local/community knowledge is actually authoritative in the study design, rather than merely cited as a sustainability framing.

Those should drive the next search round. Do not broaden the ontology unless a returned source exposes a consumer distinction the current hyperfabric cannot represent.
