# Source-attributed transformative principles for digital ESD

**Status:** candidate cross-paper synthesis for manuscript development. These principles are not claimed as empirical findings of any one Alice Brown/co-author paper, and their cross-pollination with sustainability literature does not create intervention effects. Final publication claims remain contingent on the declared structured search and screening.

## Generative logic

The paper's generative centre is:

```text
mature digital-education research corpus
        ↓ source-attributed cross-paper synthesis
candidate transferable pedagogical principles
        ↓ pedagogy / institution / professional development / policy conditions
sustainability constraints and evidence
        ↓ externality-incidence audit
candidate transformative digital-ESD framework
```

The layers remain distinct. A pedagogical insight does not create sustainability evidence; a sustainability framework does not retroactively become an Alice-source finding; naming a scaling condition does not prove that scalable implementation has occurred; and measuring an aggregate impact does not identify how that impact is distributed.

## Why these seven rather than a list of technologies

The source corpus repeatedly resists reduction to a technology inventory. The stronger recurring pattern is developmental and epistemic:

```text
proxy / engagement signal
→ restore context
→ widen participation and agency
→ route locally appropriate support
→ retain plural observers
→ return evidence
→ revise the chart
```

This pattern is compatible with the Special Issue's move from technology use toward educational transformation because it asks what changes in pedagogy, institutional practice, professional capability and governance—not merely which tool is deployed.

## Sustainability cross-pollination

The sustainability corpus adds independent constraints rather than supplying the pedagogical principles themselves:

- **environmental/material:** ICT lifecycle, service life, repair, reuse, circularity, energy, water and e-waste where applicable;
- **social:** equity, accessibility, digital exclusion, wellbeing and distribution of burdens/benefits;
- **economic:** total cost of ownership, procurement commitments and long-term support/resource requirements;
- **epistemic/governance:** participant authority, deliberation, public accountability and contestability;
- **infrastructure:** interoperability, portability, migration, open systems, repair/support windows and vendor dependence;
- **institutional/time:** professional development, policy, monitoring, integration depth, durability and longitudinal revision; and
- **intergenerational:** preservation of future learner and institutional options rather than irreversible lock-in.

The practical question for each principle is therefore not simply **"does it work?"** but:

> Who participates? Who interprets? Who benefits? Who bears the externalities? What professional and institutional capability is required? What infrastructure is committed? What can still be changed, repaired, migrated or rejected later?

## Externality incidence, not merely externality magnitude

The question **"who bears the externalities?"** is treated as a distributional audit rather than as a synonym for total environmental impact. The current formal carrier asks nine separately auditable questions:

```math
I(D) = (
  \text{contribution},
  \text{benefit},
  \text{burden},
  \text{voice},
  \text{control/mediation},
  \text{exit},
  \text{lifecycle stage},
  \text{time},
  \text{material position}
).
```

The governing no-go rules are:

```math
\boxed{
\text{aggregate outcome}
\not\Rightarrow
\{\text{contribution, benefit, burden, voice, control, exit}\}
}
```

```math
\boxed{
\text{burden bearer}
\neq
\text{causal contributor}
\neq
\text{decision controller}
\neq
\text{beneficiary}
}
```

and, temporally:

```math
\boxed{
\text{present benefit}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{later burden}.
}
```

This matters for digital education because a school, university or learner can receive a near-term educational benefit while different actors bear procurement, maintenance, workload, exclusion, surveillance, energy, material, replacement or disposal burdens at different stages and times.

### Cross-domain stress tests

The following repository domains are used as **structural calibration only**. They do not provide evidence about a digital-education deployment and do not imply a political conclusion.

| Calibration domain | Reusable failure mode | Digital-ESD question generated |
|---|---|---|
| TSMC/HBM manufacturing-demand and policy lane | Long-horizon capacity planning, customer commitments and government support do not by themselves prove a permanent chokepoint, downstream viability or realised return. | Who commits capacity and public/institutional resources, who captures the benefit, and who carries stranded/support risk if demand or technology changes? |
| Petrochemical lifecycle lane | The same broad material label cannot recover lifecycle stage; process identity does not create an emissions quantity. | At which lifecycle stage is a device/platform burden generated, and do we have a same-object measurement rather than a generic technology label? |
| Iran/Trump-energy/crack-spread lane | Upstream producer, refiner/intermediary and end user occupy different material positions; an intermediate margin does not establish end-user benefit; a market outcome does not establish unique cause or named-actor motive. | Are institutional/vendor benefits being confused with learner/household benefit, and are visible burden carriers being confused with causal responsibility? |
| Socio-technical power/selection lane | Same functional service can coexist with different power topology; broad participation does not imply distributed mediation; identical plans do not transport ecological fit. | Who controls the platform/procurement/standards layer, and does nominal participation leave meaningful influence and exit distributed? |

The point of these comparisons is diagnostic transfer:

```math
\boxed{
\text{cross-domain structural analogy}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{cross-domain empirical conclusion}.
}
```

They help expose missing coordinates in an educational analysis; they do not pay those coordinates.

## A possible future epistemic-incidence split

The Longino/Dotson/Medina acquisition lane suggests an additional diagnostic possibility around the `voice` coordinate:

```math
\text{voice present}
\neq
\text{testimony uptake}
\neq
\text{interpretive-resource adequacy}.
```

This is **not yet promoted into the digital-ESD incidence carrier**. Longino's evidential-context/criticism work, Dotson's testimony-uptake/silencing analysis and Medina's plural interpretive-resource account currently supply candidate coordinate families only. Following the least-coordinate rule, the split should be added only if an actual digital-ESD collision shows that the existing `whoHasVoice` coordinate fails to separate consumer-relevant states.

## Contribution boundary

Chugh (2026) already provides a close sustainability-paradox antecedent, and Böhme (2026) already provides a close twin-transformation / digitainability antecedent. The candidate contribution here is therefore not the bare claim that digital education should be sustainable, nor the bare claim that sustainability and digitality should be coupled.

The narrower contribution is the **source-attributed transfer architecture**: established digital-education findings are converted into candidate transformative principles while preserving source roles, context, participant authority, intervention uncertainty, scaling conditions, sustainability constraints, externality incidence and unresolved evidence dependencies.

This matrix is a candidate conceptual contribution and should be revised against the completed database search, screened corpus and structured extraction before being promoted into final review findings.
