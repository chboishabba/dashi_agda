# Source-attributed transformative principles for digital ESD

**Status:** candidate cross-paper synthesis for manuscript development. These principles are not claimed as empirical findings of any one Alice Brown/co-author paper, and their cross-pollination with sustainability literature does not create intervention effects. Final publication claims remain contingent on the declared structured search and screening.

## Generative logic

The paper's generative centre is:

```text
mature digital-education research corpus
        ↓ source-attributed cross-paper synthesis
candidate transferable pedagogical principles
        ↓ pedagogy / institution / professional development / policy conditions
sustainability constraints and evidence
        ↓
candidate transformative digital-ESD framework
```

The three layers remain distinct. A pedagogical insight does not create sustainability evidence; a sustainability framework does not retroactively become an Alice-source finding; and naming a scaling condition does not prove that scalable implementation has occurred.

## Why these seven rather than a list of technologies

The source corpus repeatedly resists reduction to a technology inventory. The stronger recurring pattern is developmental and epistemic:

```text
proxy / engagement signal
→ restore context
→ widen participation and agency
→ route locally appropriate support
→ retain plural observers
→ return evidence
→ revise the chart
```

This pattern is compatible with the Special Issue's move from technology use toward educational transformation because it asks what changes in pedagogy, institutional practice, professional capability and governance—not merely which tool is deployed.

## Sustainability cross-pollination

The sustainability corpus adds independent constraints rather than supplying the pedagogical principles themselves:

- **environmental/material:** ICT lifecycle, service life, repair, reuse, circularity, energy, water and e-waste where applicable;
- **social:** equity, accessibility, digital exclusion, wellbeing and distribution of burdens/benefits;
- **economic:** total cost of ownership, procurement commitments and long-term support/resource requirements;
- **epistemic/governance:** participant authority, deliberation, public accountability and contestability;
- **infrastructure:** interoperability, portability, migration, open systems, repair/support windows and vendor dependence;
- **institutional/time:** professional development, policy, monitoring, integration depth, durability and longitudinal revision; and
- **intergenerational:** preservation of future learner and institutional options rather than irreversible lock-in.

The practical question for each principle is therefore not simply **"does it work?"** but:

> Who participates? Who interprets? Who benefits? Who bears the externalities? What professional and institutional capability is required? What infrastructure is committed? What can still be changed, repaired, migrated or rejected later?

## Contribution boundary

Chugh (2026) already provides a close sustainability-paradox antecedent, and Böhme (2026) already provides a close twin-transformation / digitainability antecedent. The candidate contribution here is therefore not the bare claim that digital education should be sustainable, nor the bare claim that sustainability and digitality should be coupled.

The narrower contribution is the **source-attributed transfer architecture**: established digital-education findings are converted into candidate transformative principles while preserving source roles, context, participant authority, intervention uncertainty, scaling conditions, sustainability constraints and unresolved evidence dependencies.

This matrix is a candidate conceptual contribution and should be revised against the completed database search, screened corpus and structured extraction before being promoted into final review findings.
